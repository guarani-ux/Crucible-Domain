# Channel and content operations

**Start here.** Pick the work below, use the inputs listed, and return the named artifact. These are portable procedures for the Content Management Director (CNT-D), Content Management Manager (CNT-M), and content specialist (CNT-S). Marketing assesses audience and distribution. Communications verifies sensitive wording and authority. Production confirms exact media, accessible assets, and creative feasibility. The programme owner confirms programme facts. The project names the actual operator, approver, recipient, and publishing account.

**Authority.** An agent can inspect approved sources and prepare drafts, QA records and handoffs. An authorized human performs the consequential account actions in this document. Confirm the actual account's permission and allowed channel, use, version, timing and destination. Never infer access from a title, tool listing or published instructions. Asana remains read-only. These operating procedures are proposals aligned with the reviewed role and playbook material. Current Calgary Public Library account access, internal policy and approvals require project evidence.

## CH-01 · Choose channel purpose and programming

**10-second view.** Take an audience task, service goal, current content inventory, and capacity. Return a channel brief that says whom it helps, what to programme, what to stop, and what success to verify. The commissioning owner can choose from feasible options.

1. Describe one audience and its situation in observable terms. Identify what they should learn, find or do. Write the content promise as a single sentence someone could verify by watching or using the item. Note any conditions such as availability, registration, branch, age, or language.
2. Trace the journey from discovery to completion today. Search as a viewer, open the intended page or resource, and record versions, access conditions, broken or indirect paths and available alternatives. Read analytics only where access is authorized. Record which findings came from observation, reported data or inference.
3. Compare improving discovery, improving or reusing existing content, and creating a new piece. For each, estimate creative work, approvals, ongoing ownership, accessible variants and the next observable task. Check the current Calgary Public Library strategy for the mandate or commitment this advances. Translate it to a beneficiary and immediate action rather than using a strategic phrase as justification. [Calgary Public Library strategic plan](https://www.calgarylibrary.ca/about-the-library/strategic-plan)
4. Scope programming by audience need and purpose. For example, plan on-demand answers for high-intent search, a distinctive repeatable series for recurring learning, or timely programme notices for dated participation. Plan the relevant channel entry, playlist or website route, companion copy, and refresh owner.
5. Choose one commissioning action or recommend waiting for a material fact. Give the decision owner the best evidence against the proposed action, expected out-of-scope work and the next review trigger.

**Copy-ready brief.**
| Audience task | Evidence and dated path | Channel promise | Use existing or make new | Effort and upkeep | Intended next action | Owner, measure and review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Educator needs a resource for the class | Both hub and resource page observed. Labels differ | Help educators find and use the correct teaching resource | Reconcile labels and audit reuse before commissioning | Programme decision plus content review | Reach the resource in the intended viewing context | Programme owner confirms wording. Editor checks the two paths. |

**Branch.** If programme eligibility or the destination differs between current sources, hold that field and any promise that depends on it. Compare existing assets and prepare independent concepts while the owner resolves the conflict. If the audience already has a usable route, a new asset needs evidence of a distinct benefit.

## CH-02 · Programme, deliver and renew a series

**10-second view.** Take a recurring audience need, credible evidence, a format idea and capacity estimate. Return a pilot charter, episode brief and renewal threshold. A producer can plan an episode and a director can decide when to continue, revise, pause or stop.

1. Define the repeatable question the series answers, why a viewer would return, and what a complete episode gives them. List three plausible episodes using existing evidence and source access. Specify which structural beats recur and where the topic changes the pacing or treatment.
2. Identify a format that the team can sustain. Estimate research, fact checks, shoot or screen recording, edit, graphics, captions, technical QA, copy variations, publication support and maintenance per episode. Assign independent owners for programme accuracy, editorial review, production and distribution. Choose a modest pilot or modified cadence when the team cannot absorb recurring load.
3. Build one pilot packet including the episode goal, first clear payoff, two or three beats, verified source and asset list, long-form route and short standalone angle, thumbnail or cover options, accessibility inventory and planned output versions. Give each edition a version, intended use, deadline and receiver. Preserve a source worksheet, timecoded script or rundown, media inventory and cue references so the next episode can reuse the form.
4. Assign a publication workback from **first audience use**, then build in facts, rights, captions, exact cut, destination, package, operator check and post-release evidence. Confirm actual availability and approval scope. Assemble local reusable assets and keep them linked to the rights and credit records.
5. Predefine renewal criteria in terms of audience task and team load. A pilot may merit a second episode if viewers can complete the intended action and each edition clears review within the team capacity. At the review, compare confusion, accessibility misses, work time and delays with the campaign evidence. Agree on a specific change and test it next episode.

**Copy-ready charter.**
| Series promise | Repeatable beat | Pilot episodes | Access/rights | Loaded effort | Qualifying evidence | Renewal decision and owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Help adults try an everyday library tool | Name the problem → show a single task → offer the route | Three practical tasks, availability confirmed | No real patron accounts on screen without authorization | Research + review + edit + captions + package | Audience can perform the task and the team can sustain the schedule | Director reviews after pilot with producer and service owner |

**Branch.** If one episode loses an interview or an approval, choose whether independently cleared episodes may move out of order. Keep prerequisites if the series is sequential. If short-form retention rises and the downstream resource use remains flat, test the path before increasing output volume.

## CH-03 · Prepare a YouTube upload and verify release

**10-second view.** Take the authorized channel, locked media and captions, exact approved usage and audience classification, metadata and destination. Return an operator brief with all Studio fields and a versioned receipt. The authorized operator can match the approved file to the selected visibility and the intended viewer can open the right path.

1. **Prepare away from the live account.** Open the controlling cut and verify filename or ID, revision, representative playback and ending. Review captions against the exact final picture and audio timing. Compare tile and title against the first clear promise. Compile title, description with correct links and attribution, thumbnail, playlist, language, chapters if needed, elements, recipients, timing, comment treatment and visibility for each use. Leave the made-for-kids audience setting or other policy classification to the designated owner with the underlying facts. Keep the cut, captions, artwork and metadata independently versioned.
2. **Confirm account and permissions.** The authorized operator signs in to YouTube Studio, validates channel identity, and confirms their delegated role can perform the exact upload and visibility steps. If the task is drafting only or upload approval is missing, return the offline packet and access status. Check channel permissions through the authorized owner. [YouTube channel permissions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9481328)
3. **Upload only within the approved staging scope.** On desktop Studio use Create → Upload videos, choose the exact file and fill the details. Verify thumbnail and playlist, audience designation and language, where applicable. Add the matching subtitle/caption file and planned video elements as supported, then inspect the Checks findings and processing state. Review any claim or unresolved issue with the designated owner. Platform checks supplement the project's rights and accessibility reviews. Explicitly set the approved staging visibility and confirm it before saving. Private staging also requires permission to send the file to the platform. [Upload videos](https://support.google.com/youtubecreatorstudio/answer/57407) · [Add subtitles and captions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2734796)
4. **Verify the staged object.** Record the exact video ID and watch URL, state, time and operator. In an authorized preview test, watch representative opening, dialogue, densest captions/graphics and ending, and confirm the entire timeline and final CTA. Open the description link, playlist or embed in the intended context if allowed. Validate both audio and CC toggling and record any check still waiting. For a Short with a related item, only use the supported Studio control if advanced feature access and the target eligibility are confirmed. Test the actual target from that surface. [Shorts related video](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157)
5. **Release as a separate exact decision.** Confirm approving authority, exact asset and metadata version, intended audience and permitted visibility, date/time/time zone, first external use and correction owner. Human operator applies approved visibility or schedule. Verify state after save, and after publication open the exact audience path with a representative authorized viewer or signed-out test where suitable. Save evidence of what loaded, on which account or surface and when, and route discrepancies. [Video privacy settings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/157177) · [Schedule publication](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1270709)

**Copy-ready operator receipt.**
| Asset and caption version | Channel and video ID | Audience decision and owner | Title, visual and destination version | Staging/target visibility | First exposure, time zone and permission | Preview and actual open | Operator and corrections |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `[cut / captions]` | `[channel / ID]` | `[decision/ref]` | `[pack / link]` | `[private / approved next state]` | `[milestone / date / owner]` | `[surface / result / timestamp]` | `[named person / next owner]` |

**Branch.** If picture changes, determine whether the authorized revision needs a new upload. A new upload gets a new URL. Reconcile the affected recipients, embeds, descriptions, playlists, social posts and scheduled links before any dependent use. A metadata-only correction may be possible on the existing upload after scoped approval and affected link checks. [Replace or delete a video](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/55770)

## CH-04 · Separate link handoff, first external use and amplification

**10-second view.** Take a versioned asset, send/schedule plans, visibility, and the exact people or systems receiving it. Return a three-milestone ledger. The campaign coordinator can tell when readiness is actually required and whether each intended audience can open the asset.

1. Record separately when an operator or newsletter colleague needs the link, when the first external audience can use it, and when later public or paid/organic amplification starts. Each needs a timezone, named owner or owner to confirm, exact asset and facts versions, destination, and approved use.
2. Determine whether the handoff recipient requires the final package or can accept a marked copy for internal prep. Record who can authorize that handoff and what the receiver may actually do. If the recipient requires final, hold until the authorized final is delivered and receipt confirmed. Coordinate the gate for the earliest external use, even if the video will be formally published later.
3. Treat an unlisted link in a newsletter, on a web page, or in a partner toolkit as external exposure at send or placement. Anyone with the link can generally view and reshare it. Choose an approved restricted route for confidential or staff-only materials. [YouTube video privacy settings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/157177)
4. Verify the exact version and URL in the actual receiving context. Record authorized send, access, receipt and first audience-facing verification individually. If a cut changes, identify the person holding each earlier link and the approved replacement. Get acknowledgement where that receiving path requires it. Continue independently safe social drafts under their own guardrails.

**Example ledger.**
| Milestone | Recipient | Version and intended use | Gate | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tuesday noon handoff | Internal newsletter owner | Final cut and routing copy for scheduled external send | Exact version, scope and access approved before handoff | Receiver confirms link opens and intended edition |
| Wednesday educator send | Actual newsletter segment | Approved unlisted resource route | Registration information, captions, rights and earliest release permission | Test actual working link in representative recipient context |
| Friday wider rollout | Public social owner | Separately approved clips and current CTA | Each clip and destination cleared | Inspect live post, crop, captions and link |

**Branch.** Conflicting deadline interpretations go to the person with authority to choose what the recipient must have. Continue independent packages. A historical practice inquiry documented all three milestones in one campaign; the method above generalizes the lesson without carrying the case's private details.

## CH-05 · Run the channel and handle corrections

**10-second view.** Take the authorized channel inventory, queue and changed facts or reported incident. Return prioritized updates with owners, version history, live checks and follow-up dates. The receiver can determine what stays, what needs a correction, and who can make the change.

1. Inventory published and queued content by topic, audience, series/playlist, exact ID, destination, linked pages, owner, status, permission, expiry and last validated date. Review the public channel as a viewer where permitted. Flag stale homepage features, orphaned episodes, expired calls to action, failed links, outdated captions, obsolete title promises, and irrelevant search paths. Assign due dates according to impact and content lifecycle.
2. Split routine maintenance from time-sensitive correction. Capture exact public state and report time. Compare the observed issue with the controlling fact/source and capture the intended audience affected. Open a correction record for factual, accessibility, privacy, rights, audience or exposure failures. Route urgency and incident decisions to the channel and communications owners and any relevant rights or privacy owner.
3. Draft a scoped resolution and countercheck. Options may be corrected metadata, accessible replacement, redirect, approved visibility change or retirement. Before a new upload or withdrawal, identify downstream recipients and copies. Preserve evidence and ask the authorized owner to select the approach and any acknowledgement or escalation. Leave moderation actions to the approved policy and named operator.
4. After authorized action, test the exact corrected or replacement item in the intended context. Verify impacted links, embeds, captions, display states, playlists and partner copies. Record observed timing and whether the issue remains visible. Compare recurring causes next review and change the check that would have caught them.

**Copy-ready correction.** `Item ID/URL | observed state/time | expected source and version | audience/exposure | recommended action and owner | permitted scope | linked paths and recipients | post-change evidence | next check`.

**Branch.** If the old link still loads after a replacement, inspect what it serves and who still uses it. A healthy URL can resolve to an outdated item. An unknown access or privacy impact remains a hold for the affected change while review and internal corrective drafts proceed.

## CH-06 · Deliver native versions across social, web and email

**10-second view.** Take one controlling facts record and a campaign plan. Return distinct draft copy, media specifications and QA per destination. The reviewer can verify that every variant makes the same factual promise and invites an achievable next action.

1. Extract the agreed message, source-backed facts, exact eligibility, dates, audience action, privacy/rights, term preferences and destination. Mark which parts are fixed and which each surface may adapt. Choose the platform from audience behaviour and authorization for the specific campaign. Treat YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, website and newsletter as candidates whose role and features require confirmation.
2. Develop a natural first line, proof and next step per surface. A full-form YouTube piece can answer the task thoroughly and offer its continuing resource. A Short can deliver one useful idea before pointing to a verified continuation. Instagram or Facebook variants may lead with a local action in a format their audience actually sees. LinkedIn can focus on institutional purpose, partner contribution or a professional lesson if it suits the brief. Web copy emphasizes eligibility, steps, effective dates and the operational destination. Newsletter copy should preserve segment relevance and direct access to the promised action.
3. Write and package exact variants, each with handle/locale, image or cut size, intended placement, safe-zone or crop check, required alt text or selective descriptive wording, captions/transcript where needed, credited material, link behaviour and preview route. Verify actual preview and current feature availability with the operator. Preserve separately reviewed versions for partners and internal audiences.
4. Compare each surface back to the controlling facts and the actual destination. Ask whether a new viewer can understand the offer without another post, and click or otherwise complete the advertised action. If an audience action differs, document why it still serves the same campaign goal.

**Template.** `Surface and audience | viewer promise | copy and asset version | next action and tested path | approvals/rights | scheduled owner and time | live check`.

**Worked copy · hypothetical educator resource.** Use only after confirming the resource and destination. An episode description can start “Build a simple stop-motion sequence with three frames. Use the activity guide to try a second version with your class.” A short clip can open “Three frames can change how a story feels. Try moving the object a little more each time.” An educator newsletter can say “Planning a storytelling activity? Open the stop-motion guide for setup, materials, and a classroom activity.” A web task label can say “Open the stop-motion activity guide,” with the relevant format and prerequisites alongside. Verify that the landing page actually offers each promised item. Remove grade or availability claims while those facts are held.

**Branch.** If dates change, find every affected variant and destination link by ID. Hold or revise only the affected promise. Leave independent evergreen material in the queue when its scope and evidence still hold.

## CH-07 · Prepare accessible CMS and staff-only editions

**10-second view.** Take copy, media and destination scope. Return a versioned preview pack, access matrix and checks. A named authorized reader can open the exact intended edition and complete the task.

1. Confirm purpose, date, audience, destination and rights for the edition. Identify data and media needing restricted access. Keep staff-only preview and publication within the approved workspace. Use a separately cleared public edition if needed.
2. Draft a descriptive title, summary, meaningful headings and link text, steps, audience-specific contact route and effective/expiry dates. Add appropriate image descriptions and ensure media has the agreed caption, transcript or visual-description route. Inspect interactive flows and page or embed behaviour in context. [W3C media accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/)
3. Inspect the exact CMS preview when accessible. Test representative desktop and mobile use, reading order and keyboard access where supported, link destinations, media playback, captions, and whether the intended viewer can navigate to the next action. An authorized representative staff viewer verifies restricted access and usability. Check a denied route only where permitted.
4. Record exact edition, differences, access/receipt outcomes, remaining checks, owner and permitted use. Human publishes or retires within the assigned scope and repeats affected checks on the live edition.

**Access matrix.** `Edition/version | audience/permission | destination | authorized representative tester | task and expected result | actual evidence | next owner/expiry`.

**Branch.** For a host-approved draft with one held section, provide only the permitted marked edition. Track affected recipient copies through the authorized final and confirmed receipt.

## CH-08 · Measure and choose the next experiment

**10-second view.** Take the campaign objective, exact items, comparable periods, authorized data and operational observations. Return a decision memo showing what to keep, change, or check next. The decision owner can trace the evidence, confounders and stop conditions.

1. Set one intended audience result before extracting metrics. Use an observable task such as opening a teaching pack, finding a branch resource, or registering where spaces are actually available. Capture a baseline, relevant denominators, measured time windows, intended surfaces, exact versions, attribution limits and privacy constraints.
2. Pull authorized reports by content type, source and period. Inspect impressions and CTR for packaging distribution and appeal where those metrics apply. Inspect retention moments, average view duration, Shorts viewer engagement and traffic sources in their defined report contexts. Use registration or service data only within authorized access and interpret availability and outside promotion explicitly. [YouTube content performance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)
3. Compare equivalent windows and formats. Separate external newsletter or campaign pushes, timing, topic, paid activity, changes in available places, and navigation or eligibility problems. For combined CTR, use totals from compatible denominators when available. Clearly label incomplete data and avoid treating every view as a service outcome.
4. Make one actionable hypothesis. High impressions with weaker clicks may justify a clearer title or image if viewers receive the advertised payoff. Strong viewing with few meaningful downstream actions warrants verifying the destination before changing the edit. A specific tool-led test can be handed to the authorized account operator only after confirming current feature and account eligibility. [YouTube title and thumbnail testing](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400)
5. Set an observation window, change one meaningful factor or record where confounding is unavoidable, and define success/stop rules. At review, record result, uncertainty, next decision, owner and any practice changes that should appear in the next brief or series playbook.

**Decision row.** `Goal / item and version / window and comparison / observed metric and denominator / task evidence / alternate explanations / next change and owner / review date and stop rule`.

**Example.** A newsletter-driven view spike proves a difference in observed distribution. If educator resource use is unknown, track an authorized downstream measure or observe the resource journey before recommending another promo. A broken CTA warrants correction and a fresh receiving check first.

## CH-09 · Prepare a segmented newsletter and preview

**Take** the message, planned segment, sender, scoped permission and source-backed destinations. **Return** a dated subject, preheader, body/CTA, audience scope and QA/test preview checklist. **Accept when** the newsletter owner can recognize the intended audience and complete the same action from the exact preview before any audience send.

1. Confirm the authorized list owner, segment purpose, exclusions, time zone and effective delivery window. Use aggregate cohort labels and approved consent/access information. Keep individual contact data in the authorized system. Distinguish test audience, actual list and copied partner audience. Unknown scope holds the send.
2. Draft a descriptive subject, preheader that adds one detail, two to four short body lines, a meaningful link label and approved sender information. Put eligibility, deadlines and limits where a reader can act. For an unlisted companion video, check the cut, captions, permitted use and earliest outside access before the link goes to anyone who can forward it.
3. Prepare a preview for the authorized sender. Verify link route and source tracking when approved, mobile readability, plain text/rendering where relevant, image alternatives, and whether the precise destination works for a representative recipient. Ask the actual sender to verify mail system settings and permission before any test send or launch. Log test receipt and production send separately.
4. If a destination or date changes, reconcile copies already with senders and partners. Return a corrected preview, version and scoped next test. In post-send review, distinguish working access, engagement reporting and service completion.

**Copy and QA row:** segment and authority | subject | preheader | body version | CTA and exact inspected destination | preview/test recipients | publication permission and time | receiving or send evidence.

## CH-10 · Pilot a complete public information or educator resource

**Take** an audience task, one representative item, confirmed content owner, dependent download or programme path and its audience. **Return** one checked page-to-resource reference edition with a decision on replication. **Accept when** the intended user can find the correct page, identify what it offers and use the complete asset on an ordinary device.

1. Build the smallest complete path before scaling: entry, responsive page, resource or download, and next action. Confirm field labels, programme scope and asset authority with the service owner. Mark a dependent asset approval independently even when it lives on a different board.
2. Inspect the ordinary receiving route and mobile layout. Try downloading or playing the exact artifact, using the same journey available to the intended user. Compare one complete download with segmented files when users need a single ready-to-use resource. Record the access and accessibility evidence for the chosen edition.
3. Log defects and decisions before cloning the template. Record CTA or tracking ownership, expected use, version of each dependency, and update or retirement owner. After replication, sample each type and test changed routes. A working page built from unapproved or outdated attachments stays provisional.

**Reference edition row:** entry | page and version | download/embed and authority | task and result | receiving device/access | decision and defect | next variants with check owner.
