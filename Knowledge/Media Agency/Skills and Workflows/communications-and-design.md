# Communications, graphic design and motion practice

## Open the work you need

| Request | Start here | Hand back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A missing service fact, a stakeholder request or a proposed campaign | CD-01 · Discover and verify; CD-02 · Secure a decision | Source-linked fact sheet and decision record |
| Leadership position, staff update, statement or media ask | CD-03 · Write and advise | Message brief and appropriately scoped draft |
| A disruption, error or community comment needs routing | CD-04 · Triage and respond | Impact log, draft response and updated versions |
| A designer needs direction or has to make a static asset | CD-05 · Brief the design; CD-06 · Build and check | Visual brief, editable source and checked variants |
| Graphics need to move or work with picture, captions and voice | CD-07 · Plan and verify motion | Timed storyboard, playtest and source/export package |
| People disagree or a reviewed deliverable changes | CD-08 · Resolve feedback and hand off | Versioned decision, delta record and receiving check |

These practices work from supplied or authorized sources and an accessible editor. Run them in an assignment with a named human decision owner and a receiver. Drafts are for the authorized audience. Keep Asana read-only. Use Calgary Public Library in full, and confirm current brand files, local responsibilities and approval scope before applying them.

## CD-01 · Discover stakeholder needs and verify claims · Skill

**Use this when** a stakeholder asks for coverage or a message that depends on an uncertain programme fact. **Return** a one-page constraint and fact sheet, a narrow question for the responsible owner and a suggested way forward. **Done when** a reviewer can point to the exact fact, what it applies to and how to correct the read-back.

1. **Write the request as a decision.** Capture requester, expected audience, real-world action, time/use, implied format and why now. Ask what would be different if the work succeeds. An initial request for a video may reveal that a link, orientation or timing change solves the problem.
2. **Map the stakeholder views.** Ask the requester about desired change, the service owner about actual access and exceptions, front-line staff about perceived barriers, the marketer about audience and placement, Production about evidence and cost, and Communications about claims and institutional voice. Route formal approval to the person who can grant it.
3. **Recover the exact evidence.** Start with the current authoritative page or record appropriate to the question. Capture date observed, effective period, exact URL, task or version, and the phrase or section. Use older or proposed records to understand the issue, with status explicit. Use public strategy for direction and a current service owner for the project-specific claim.
4. **Write one confirmable question.** Specify the disputed words, proposed audience, destination and decision deadline. Ask “For the educator hub and this standalone pack, what grades and subject labels are current, what exceptions matter, and where should caregivers or educators be sent?” Ask what can be shown publicly and who can clear it separately.
5. **Read back the answer.** Rewrite it as one proposed claim with its scope, existing qualifiers, source and effective date. Show the service owner the exact phrasing or image implication to confirm. Update the evidence state only when there is evidence of that confirmation.
6. **Return the supported portion now.** Mark the affected phrase or call to action held. Finish grounded material, neutral layouts and a route request that keeps questionable content out of released assets.

**Copy-ready claim row:** claim ID · draft wording · exact wording and locator · effective date · applies to · exceptions · verified, reported, inferred, proposed, unknown or contradicted · fact owner and response due · intended use · affected artifacts · confirmation receipt.

**Worked example.** The public Educators hub and Storytelling Through Stop Motion resource use different grade and subject labels in the checked snapshot. Carry both as dated observations. Keep neutral copy and layout preparation moving while the service owner determines whether the pages address distinct uses or one needs updating. Affected public wording and targeted call to action stay held. [Educators hub](https://www.calgarylibrary.ca/connect/educators) · [Stop Motion resource](https://www.calgarylibrary.ca/connect/educators/stop-motion)

## CD-02 · From intake to a decision meeting · Workflow

**Use this when** a kickoff, resource commitment or direction change requires agreement. **Return** a short pre-read, decision agenda, decision and dependency log. **Done when** the coordinator can recover who decided, which version and constraints govern, and what each next worker can start.

1. **Prepare from CD-01.** In the first lines state recommendation, decision needed today, audience benefit, decision owner and true deadline. Distinguish the review deadline, partner handoff, first audience access and public amplification when applicable.
2. **Give comparable choices.** Include the recommended approach, a credible lighter approach and the consequence of doing nothing now. For each, spell out audience impact, effort, dependencies, rights or staffing risks, and what would reverse the preference.
3. **Frame the ask for each receiver.** Ask a service owner to confirm eligibility and availability. Ask Marketing to choose the primary audience and test the next action. Ask Production to assess what can actually be captured, designed or delivered. Ask Communications for message and representation constraints. Ask the named sponsor about priority or resources only within their remit.
4. **Run or prepare the meeting.** One versioned pre-read. Example 20-minute proposed agenda: 3 minutes on facts and audience, 5 on options, 7 on unresolved tradeoffs, 5 for the owner’s decision and read-back. If the owner is absent or authority uncertain, get the information and prepare the decision request. Keep the decision state pending.
5. **Read back and assign.** Record exact decision, allowed use, affected artifact, open conditions, owner, time, review point and downstream check. Route a disagreement to a specific person and identify parallel unblocked work. If a staffing proposal or outreach is still open, give it an owner for confirmation and mark the commitment as proposed.
6. **Reconcile changes.** A later direction change updates the scoped decision and the versions that depend on it. Trace past and outstanding copies. Ask the next recipient to identify the exact controlling directive before acting.

For a cross-listed programme promotion request, keep one task identity and record each placement's deliverable, owner and review gate. A practical route is brief → subject expert → internal creative review → external creative review where assigned → web/social/print owners. An approved direction may still receive a partner wording change. Route that change back to the designer and identify the affected source layout, variants and approval instead of copying a second task or leaving the design proof on old copy.

**Decision record:** Decision date and due time | requested decision | option chosen and why | authority + basis | artifact/version | audience/use | required rechecks and holds | next person/actions | read-back evidence.

**Worked example.** A campaign asks for a complex teaser, but the owner has a proven reusable illustration and timing is short. Put “approve the simple pilot direction and scope by Thursday” at top, show a lightweight cut and higher-effort shoot, ask production about rights and capacity and ask Marketing whether the simple asset still enables the desired action. Delivery timing and approval state remain separate.

## CD-03 · Frame institutional posture and write for leaders, staff or press · Skill

**Use this when** an authorized team needs a decision memo, executive message, staff briefing, key message or media response. **Return** a scoped direction note and the requested versions. **Done when** the owner can verify each consequential claim, the action and the intended audience.

1. **Select the document and action.** A leadership memorandum asks for a decision. A staff briefing explains what changes in practice. A media holding line confirms what can responsibly be said now and says where further information will appear. For an on-camera executive script, make the logic speakable and defer timed audio/picture mechanics to the A/V practice.
2. **Build a message spine.** State one sentence of known change, one of audience consequence, one of next action. Underneath, attach evidence and material qualifiers. Record authorized audience, risk, urgency, tone and what may be shared. For strategy links, show how the work serves an actual mandate and audience need using the effective plan.
3. **Write for the surface.** Decision memo order: ask → recommendation → options → consequence → decision owner and due time. Staff note order: what changed → who is affected → immediate actions → where staff route public questions → next update. Press draft order: confirmed fact → effect → useful action → approved contact or next update → optional verified context. Draft a short Q&A for likely questions that exceed the main text.
4. **Run a factual and editorial read.** Verify names, dates, promises, statistics, quotation and intended audience treatment with their owners. Retain material caveats and remove filler; ask whether a literal reading implies more than the evidence supports. An executive quote remains proposed language until that person or assigned owner approves it.
5. **Choose the receiving routes.** COM-S drafts, COM-M aligns versions and routes, and COM-D or the designated authority decides sensitive posture. Media Centre gives the public inquiry route. Verify the particular assignment's authorized spokesperson and publishing arrangements.

**Copy-ready draft header:** document type and version · decision or action at top · approved/proposed message scope · audience/channel · priority fact owner · approval owner · first permitted use · known holds · source links.

**Illustrative holding line:** “Some booking links are currently unavailable. Please check [verified service status destination] before travelling. We’ll update that page [approved update timing].” A newsroom deadline changes priority, while facts, wording and distribution still need their scoped approvals. [Calgary Public Library Media Centre](https://www.calgarylibrary.ca/about-the-library/media-centre) · [Strategic Plan](https://www.calgarylibrary.ca/about-the-library/strategic-plan)

## CD-04 · Triage a disruption, correction or community referral · Workflow

**Use this when** a report, social comment, media inquiry or service disruption raises a concern. **Return** a restricted impact log, response option, correction map and check-in route. **Done when** a named owner can decide what to do, find all affected copies, and check the public or staff outcome when allowed.

1. Capture time received, original link/version or authorized location, what the person reports, urgency and which audience might be affected. Keep personal details in approved systems. Record an inquiry’s actual deadline and source; a screenshot or comment is a report until verified.
2. Classify and route: routine access question → service or approved reply route; demonstrable factual error → service fact owner and publishing owner; sensitive privacy, safety, cultural or reputational matter → relevant institutional response route; proposed moderation → current channel policy and designated reviewer. Separate harm, immediacy and reversibility from sentiment. If severity is unclear, route as unresolved and avoid publishing a definitive interpretation.
3. Establish current service facts and what remains unknown. Ask the decision owner to choose between an update, temporary holding line, scoped correction or referral. Prepare accessible wording that acknowledges the impact and gives a practical next action. Record whether the first response is public, staff-only or individually routed and who can send it.
4. Trace affected places: controlling page, social post, programme listing, queued newsletter, branch script, partner copy, thumbnail claim or edited video. Assign each channel owner a specific check and record what can continue. Hold changes to official channels for the relevant authority.
5. For an authorized correction, prepare exact old/new wording, destination and time, then verify the visible result and recipient receipt when available. Keep outdated copies recognizable as superseded. If some versions cannot be amended, route a pinned correction, replacement or direct recipient notice for the appropriate owner. Recheck the next update and close with what was actually observed.

**Impact log row:** received at · channel/item and exact version · reported issue · verified impact and source · unknowns · audience and affected copies · owner and decision due · proposed response and approved scope · next check · outcome or hold.

**Worked branch.** A public post links to an expired registration page. Confirm expiry and the new destination first. Draft corrected copy and a handoff. Keep future related posts parked while destination-dependent wording is checked. If exposed audience data is alleged, preserve authorized evidence, minimize access and route to the designated response owner. [CDC crisis and risk communication principles, background for clear public responses](https://www.cdc.gov/cerc/media/pdfs/CERC_Introduction.pdf)

## CD-05 · Translate a content brief into a visual brief · Skill

**Use this when** an asset needs a design direction or exact instructions for a graphic or motion designer. **Return** a scoped creative brief, content hierarchy, visual options and a review question. **Done when** a maker can start and the reviewer can judge the proof against a real audience action.

1. Define the first moment: who sees it, what environment and placement, what they must recognize, what they can do next and how much attention they plausibly have. State the title/title pairing or adjacent copy. Treat the graphic and its destination as a single journey.
2. List exact approved copy and source, plus unresolved qualifications or facts. Divide copy into must-see, supporting and accessible nearby text. Select the dominant element. For a dense service graphic, keep the single action and move details to the accessible destination.
3. Recover current authorized brand files, logos, fonts, palettes and approvals. Lock licensed imagery and actual use for each placement. A public logo-request route supports retrieval; obtain the release authority separately.
4. Sketch two treatments with real content. For example, one portrait crop using a hands-on image and one typographic treatment for when imagery is held. Compare visual relevance, legibility, accessible alternative, effort to create variants, and actual cut-off or safe-area risks.
5. Specify primary and alternate surfaces, required sizes or current specification source, source/editability, accessibility artifacts, deadlines, exact receiver, and what reviewers are deciding now. A complex or motion proof should reach reviewers before delivery renders.

**Visual brief fields:** audience and action · placement and first-use context · approved words and sources · must-see hierarchy · candidate visuals and rights · brand files · dimensions and safe areas by surface · access and alt-text needs · editable and exported objects · review decision · owner and due date · acceptance test.

**Illustrative example.** A family activity asset asks caregivers to open an existing guide. Present one cue for the activity and a legible title. Put eligibility and session details in live text with the source and date. Prepare a neutral typographic layout when the photograph has unresolved reuse permission. [Calgary Public Library Media Centre](https://www.calgarylibrary.ca/about-the-library/media-centre) · [Library accessibility](https://www.calgarylibrary.ca/your-library/accessibility)

## CD-06 · Make static graphics and thumbnails · Skill

**Use this when** social, a web article, a partner package or a channel upload needs a static visual. **Return** the editable source plus per-surface image files, alt-text guidance and a checked manifest. **Done when** the receiver can open and modify the source, identify the correct placement file and see what was checked.

1. Lay out the key subject, headline and action from CD-05. Use an underlying grid and alignment shared across variants. Establish hierarchy through meaningful size, weight, contrast and space. Reserve the strongest emphasis for the first decision. Keep a short or alternate text block for small previews.
2. Make the authentic subject visible even without type. Avoid crops that misstate who a programme is for or obscure what a viewer needs to see. In a thumbnail, check image and title together at feed size and compare with the video’s actual opening and payoff. A click can help only if the promise holds.
3. Inspect native target previews and representative devices. Check actual crop, overlay locations, contrast at minimum size and over the poorest part of image backgrounds. Check the provider’s current thumbnail options in the intended account and surface. Save the preview observed and specs checked. If you lack access, give the operator an exact preview check and mark delivery QA pending.
4. Make the essential information available in adjacent text. Decide whether each image is informative, functional, complex or decorative in its actual context, and provide an appropriate text alternative or placement guidance. For web WCAG 2.2 AA contrast checks, use the applicable 4.5:1 normal-text or 3:1 large-text threshold and check exceptions; also test actual legibility. Colour alone cannot indicate the only relevant distinction.
5. Export from the exact editable source version. Verify dimensions, profile, transparency where required, filenames, current platform specification and reopened files. Include fonts, links and rights/credit references as permitted. Check approved visibility and reviewer receipt before any publication or partner use.

**Variant row:** filename + version · placement/crop · title/caption pairing · key content and alt text · source and rights · exact export parameters · preview and accessibility result · allowed use · handoff owner and receipt.

**Make/review exercise.** For “Make your first stop-motion scene,” compare a genuine frame of the activity with a generic book photo. The reviewer checks which communicates the actual activity at mobile size, and whether the title still reads when the destination applies its crop. Return the proof and the reason for the chosen treatment. [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) · [W3C alt-text decision tree](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/decision-tree/) · [YouTube thumbnail help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431)

## CD-07 · Plan, build and check motion graphics · Skill

**Use this when** animated text, data, an explainer beat, title or lower third adds meaning to video. **Return** a timed storyboard, representative playback proof, editable sources, exact exports and release checks. **Done when** the editor can inspect the densest moment with all layers and tell what reopens if picture, claims or timing changes.

1. Start with placement, current picture version, intended viewer task, approved words/facts, reference screen crops, soundtrack and caption plan. Choose the reason for motion: reveal order, connect cause and effect, locate change or identify a person. Begin with a static proof when movement adds no decision value.
2. Build a beat sheet with timeline in/out, exact copy, image and transition, audio, caption relationship and use scope. Sketch a frame for the beginning, densest overlap and destination-specific endpoint. Name source and credit for charts or illustrations. Confirm the playback time budget with an actual read-through.
3. Make a low-cost timed proof. Use a neutral sound placeholder if audio is held and label its limits. Test muted and with audio. Add final-position captions and realistic interface overlays in preview. Check which element receives attention first. Extend a reading hold, shorten copy or sequentially reveal content when simultaneous speech, captions and graphics compete. For platform-specific safe areas, verify actual UI and target cuts. Title-safe guides help frame a proof.
4. Check text contrast over moving backgrounds, caption position, names and sequence, reading time, essential information visible through cuts and crop, and potential flashing or motion concerns. Route missing policy or accessibility expertise to the responsible reviewer. If a portrait version crops meaningful content, make a distinct layout and proof.
5. Receive review at exact proof version. Pin feedback to time and scope. If picture timing changes, recheck captions, music and effects cues, graphic holds and all dependent exports. A copy-only change triggers a wording, layout and affected-authority review. Carry separate lower thirds/alpha assets, fonts or dependencies, editable project and accessible rendered variant when the spec requires.

**Timed row:** 00:00–00:04 · fact/approved copy source · visual state and safe area · motion and hold · voice/music · caption area · blocked or confirmed item · reviewer and outcome.

| Proposed sequence | What plays | What the editor verifies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 00:00–00:03 | Establish the action with a clean close-up and unobtrusive sound. | Viewers can see the item and where the next instruction will apply. |
| 00:03–00:07 | Show the step while the narration explains it. Reserve the caption area and avoid a second instructional graphic. | Caption wording and framing leave the actual demonstrated action visible. |
| 00:07–00:11 | Hold the brief benefit or next-step graphic in a speech pause. | Proof audience can read it once on the actual target preview, with no premature cut. |

**Worked risk.** In a service video, a benefit statement, narrated explanation and captions all compete at the same second. Prepare one proof with the statement delayed to a quiet beat and one with essential copy simplified. The receiving viewer identifies the benefit before anyone explains it. This adapts a discipline from the campaign review summarized in the internal playbook. [After Effects safe margins guidance](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/get-started/preferences-and-settings/preferences.html) · [W3C media accessibility planning](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/)

## CD-08 · Resolve competing feedback and hand off accessible versions · Workflow

**Use this when** a design, statement or video is in review, or a changed asset has already travelled. **Return** a feedback ledger, revised exact-version manifest and access/receipt evidence. **Done when** the next person can act on the authorized version and identify the remaining holds.

1. Issue one review file or link with purpose, current version, open question, deadline and audience. Label draft, targeted review or approved use. Obtain approval of facts, institution voice, brand, accessibility, participant use and release through their owners.
2. Convert feedback into rows: version/time range, requested change, required/suggested/question/approval, owner, conflict, decision date and closure evidence. Trace a conflicting opinion to the person who owns that decision. Preserve helpful ideas outside the active change when possible.
3. Reproduce and verify fixes at the affected moments and in representative actual placements. Log what each change invalidates. Treat unverified playback, contact, access, delivery and release as separate status. Keep source files and versions aligned with the receiver.
4. Provide exact versions, contents, recipient permissions, change summary, QC results and known holds. If an internal review-only copy is authorized, mark what its receiver can do. Final-required use begins when the authorized final is received and verified. Where access is pending, share only the needed authorized information and route the check to the named owner.
5. Compare outcome with intent. Capture one lesson, its source, limits and a future test. A repeat problem such as re-opening caption timing merits a proposal to change the earlier script, proof or review method.

**Receipt:** approved artifact and scope · delivery destination · source/editability · variants and accessibility · dependency rechecks · recipient/version · access verified · recipient action confirmed · open risks and owner.

## Evidence and qualification

These are enactable practice instructions, grounded in the source-linked Media Agency role research, its [verification record](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1wk8GQjT-5pEoBaOAvYnR5PUYuXzNAqgT/view?usp=drivesdk), Work Operating System playbooks, and a current read-only Asana review.
