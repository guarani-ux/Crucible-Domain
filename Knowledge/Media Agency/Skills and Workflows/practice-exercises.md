## Exercise 1 · Writing

### Fold and Share

#### Decision brief · v0.1 · Internal review

**Decision requested by proposed Day 1.** Approve a 30-second, hands-only 16:9 demonstration for the public programme page that shows caregivers and children ages 8–10 how to make a crease and draw a character with paper and pencil. The earliest proposed audience access is Day 6, after an authorized release owner clears the final cut and page placement.

##### 1. Project Context

**Background.** Fold and Share is a fictional beginner library session. The scenario supplies the audience, materials, placement and production constraints. The programme owner confirms the learning objective. A registration URL remains unverified.

**Opportunity.** A first-time viewer may need to see what it means to press a fold and how the resulting line can guide a drawing. This is a working hypothesis. If a simple written instruction lets beginners find the crease and start their own character as reliably, reconsider the need for video.

##### 2. Goal & Objectives

**Primary goal.** After one viewing, a caregiver or child can use one sheet of household paper and a pencil to make a crease and use it as a guide for a character of their own.

**Check.** In an authorized preview, ask a beginner to demonstrate how to make the crease and show where they would start the drawing. Run the same check with audio muted. If a viewer misses where the crease comes from, hold the fold and reopening on screen longer before testing again.

**Content considerations.** The campaign manager reviews the exact spoken and on-screen wording. A registration invitation and link stay held until the destination and wording are verified for use. Proposed connection to Calgary Public Library’s Experiences priority is a hands-on creative activity. Programme owner to validate that connection if it is used in internal planning.

##### 3. Tone & Audience

**Audience.** Caregivers and children ages 8–10 encountering the activity for the first time, potentially together on a phone. Use short, concrete language and leave time to see where the edges meet.

**Tone and visual reference.** Practical and playful. Begin with the demonstrator’s finished character, then show the same simple construction from above against a plain surface. An example face gives the viewer a starting point for inventing their own character.

**Key message.** One fold gives you a line to build a character around.

##### 6. Talent Summary & Production Specs

**Coverage.** Capture a complete fold-to-drawing take, a clean hold on the finished sheet and a backup crease close-up. Use soft side light to make the crease visible. Keep the action near frame centre where it preserves the primary composition. If the crease is hard to see, adjust side light or move the camera closer and capture the opening action again with the same orientation. The activity uses only household paper and pencil.

#### A/V script · v0.1 · Pre-shoot draft for internal review

**Purpose and audience.** Give caregivers and children ages 8–10 a complete first attempt at folding a crease and turning it into a drawn character. **Format.** 0:30, 16:9, public programme page, with captions. **Source basis.** Fictional Fold and Share scenario and proposed original demonstration footage of the cleared adult’s hands. VO and text are authored draft. The programme owner confirms the learning objective and the campaign manager reviews language. Release approval is pending assignment of an owner.

| Section | Audio | Video |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 · 0:00–0:05 · Show the result | **VO.** “This face started with one fold.” Leave the remaining time for the viewer to see the drawing. | Open on the finished original sheet from directly above. A simple face straddles the vertical crease. Hold steady so the face and line are visible. **On-screen.** `Fold and Share`. Cut to a blank sheet at 0:05. |
| 02 · 0:05–0:13 · Make the fold | **VO.** “Bring the left edge to the right. Press along the fold.” Start each action on its cue. Let the paper sound come through at the end if clean. | Locked overhead. Adult hands bring the left edge to the right edge of a landscape sheet, align the corners, then press a finger down the folded edge and lift it so the fold is visible. **On-screen.** `Match the edges. Press the fold.` Continue on the same shot. |
| 03 · 0:13–0:17 · Find the crease | **VO.** “Open the paper. That line is your crease.” Leave the end of the beat for the open sheet. | Same overhead and sheet orientation. Adult hands open and flatten the sheet, then point to the vertical line with a finger beside it. Keep the crease in focus and hold through 0:17. **On-screen.** `Open to see the crease.` Continue on the same shot. |
| 04 · 0:17–0:24 · Draw the face | **VO.** “Draw an eye on each side. Add a mouth.” Leave room to see the mouth appear. | Same overhead. The adult draws one dot on each side of the crease and a curved mouth below. The hands move out of frame. The finished face matches the opening shot. **On-screen.** `Add eyes and a mouth.` Continue into the closing hold. |
| 05 · 0:24–0:30 · Invite a try | **VO.** “Take a sheet of paper and a pencil. Try your own character.” Let the final frame hold through 0:30. | Hold the same finished drawing with the pencil visible beside the sheet. **On-screen.** `Try your own character` and, below, `Paper and pencil`. Keep the end card visible for the full six seconds. Cut out at 0:30. |

**Timing estimate.** At an assumed 150 words per minute, the 46 spoken words occupy 18.4 seconds across the five sections. Remaining time by section is approximately 2.6, 3.6, 0.8, 3.4 and 1.2 seconds. Picture action overlaps speech. The edit uses cuts and continuations within the stated windows. Music and added effects are intentionally absent. Time a full spoken read and paper rehearsal before script lock. In the four-second reopening beat, check that the crease is visible with enough time to recognize it. Shift time from the drawing hold if the reopening needs longer.

**Access and review.** Captions follow the accepted voice-over and any meaningful sound. Keep captions clear of the crease, drawing and instructional text, then check the muted playback at phone size. Proposed accompanying description for the programme page: “Bring the left edge of a sheet of paper to the right edge. Press down the fold. Open the paper and look for the crease. Draw an eye on each side of the crease and a mouth underneath, or invent your own character.” Bind caption timings to the accepted cut and recheck them after any timing change. Review the optional vertical crop against the complete fold and the legibility of both text layers before committing to it.

#### Setup and recorded checks

- **Camera A, portrait 9:16 proposed.** Lock a slightly elevated view with the full starting sheet, both hands throughout the fold and the finished result readable at portrait playback size. Include the presenter’s face if the same frame keeps the action and focus readable. If it cannot, set A for the presenter’s hands and carry their cleared voice on the verified lav path.
- **Camera B, horizontal 16:9 proposed.** Lock a three-quarter view on the same side of the paper, with the same full action and a cleared background. With both cameras ready, the producer starts B first, then A, then calls the slate and action. Check that both are recording before the cue. The sound helper monitors audio through the take. Log actual recording IDs, frame rates, picture profiles and audio track assignments.
- **Sound test.** Route the supplied lav into a verified record input and monitor that recording path. At 09:40 record 20–30 seconds including the presenter’s normal cue, a louder repetition, reaching and leaning through the fold, the paper crease, and five seconds of quiet. Listen to the saved clip through headphones. Every word should be intelligible and the paper should be audible without masking the cue. Check for clipped peaks, lav rub, hum or HVAC masking, dropouts, and sync at the slate and at the fold. With a dBFS meter, start near −12 dBFS for normal speech peaks and leave headroom for the louder cue. If audio is separate from either picture, show an audible slate to both cameras and verify sync at the finish too. If sound fails, address the observed cause by adjusting gain, reseating the lav or moving away from noise with room approval, then record and listen again. Log the test clip and actual path.
- **Coverage test.** Play the saved test from each intended camera, at its intended orientation. Check start sheet, reach, alignment, crease and final hold. Both hands, moving paper edges and the crease must remain visible and sharp. Check recorded exposure, flicker, background and caption space while the hands are moving. Record pass or hold by camera. A failed portrait view gets reframed and replayed before the main take. Use verified alternate placement, light or battery power if available. A room/access or safety failure, uncleared person or background, unavailable safe power or light, or lack of a playable readable portrait and intelligible recorded voice holds the affected capture or use. The producer owns the pickup decision if an authorised correction cannot be verified before the presenter leaves.
