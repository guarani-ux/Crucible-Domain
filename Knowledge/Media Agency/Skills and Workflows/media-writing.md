# Writing for public-service media

**Start here.** Pick the document by the decision it serves. Use **MW-01** to diagnose the ask and define the assignment, **MW-02** to make an idea visible, **MW-03** to win a bounded pitch or design a series, **MW-04** to write the full audiovisual sequence, **MW-05** to adapt and package copy, **MW-06** to write for an executive or host, and **MW-07** to reconcile reviews. Each can be run from this file using the project sources. The workflows at the end join the procedures into repeatable delivery paths.

## MW-01 · Write a project or media production brief

**Take:** Request, known audience need, current programme or source material, real destination and launch constraints, decision roles.  
**Return:** One completed brief with a one- or two-sentence top decision summary, seven parts, scoped unknowns and disposition.  
**Pass:** In ten seconds a reader can identify the audience, desired change, proposed output, earliest consequential date and decision needed. A requester can correct the diagnosis and a producer can derive assignments from the body.

1. Extract deadlines and deliverables with their actual uses. Distinguish handoff, first audience access and public amplification. Recover decisions from the applicable source and version. For each conflict, record both claims and who can settle it.
2. Diagnose the barrier. State who is trying to do what, what evidence shows friction, the observable change, and what could disprove your explanation. If destination, eligibility or capacity blocks action, propose a route repair, verified alternative or lower-risk information piece before increasing reach. If seeing an action materially helps the audience, name what they must see.
3. Ask the person who controls the service a focused question. Provide exact competing wording, date, intended audience and use. Read the answer back as a candidate line with qualifiers. Capture fact verification separately from clearance to publish. If contact is outside your scope, draft the question for the authorized coordinator. Track Marketing’s audience and placement, Communications’ facts and institutional wording, Production’s feasibility, and the project or executive authority for decisions in their scope only.
4. Put the summary into the opening metadata or handoff note without altering the seven numbered headings. Complete the body with concise finished sentences. Give each deliverable a distinct audience job. Enter `TBD — owner: [role]` for operational gaps and state what may continue.
5. Test hollow success. If every asset could be produced while the real goal still fails, repair the objective, destination or rollout. Resolve any deadline ambiguity against the output it governs.

**Copy this scaffold.** Retain the supplied current team template where available. The example rows identify likely decisions and can be tailored to approved scope.

| Part | Complete these fields |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Project Context** | **Background:** timely verified situation and current path. **Opportunity:** audience barrier, rationale and what would reverse the diagnosis. |
| **2. Goal & Objectives** | **Primary Goal:** one observable audience change. **Objectives:** supporting outcomes and how they will be checked. **Mandatory Content Considerations:** authorized language, evidence, review, accessibility and holds. |
| **3. Tone & Audience** | **Audience Profile:** situation, motivation, prior knowledge, access. **Tone Keywords:** linked to writing or scene decisions. **Visual Reference / Moodboard:** usable evidence/reference. **Key Messages:** plain audience-facing claims. |
| **4. Deliverables & Technical Specs** | Use Asset Type, Ratio, Runtime, Purpose. Include applicable main feature, social cutdowns and accessibility assets. Route exact specifications to the confirming owner. |
| **5. How This Video Will Be Used** | **Distribution Channels:** which versions go where, who owns placement and the next action. **Context of Viewing:** device, sound, timing, account/access, shelf life and verified destination. |
| **6. Talent Summary & Production Specs** | **Talent Requirements, Locations, Visual Style:** real availability, permissions and fallback, distinct from creative suggestions. |
| **7. Timeline & Stakeholder Milestones** | Use Milestone, Target Date, Role, Stakeholder. Identify script/concept lock, shoot, buffer, first cut, review, final export, final approval, recipient handoff and go-live as applicable. Name approval scope and the dependencies behind counters. |

**Example opening (illustrative).** “Educators deciding whether to try stop-motion need one accurate way to assess the activity and find the resource. Decide whether to repair the resource path before producing a proposed 30-second demonstration. Programme owner to confirm audience scope before any external copy.”  
**Disposition:** READY FOR APPROVAL when a complete brief can be reviewed, RESEARCH FIRST when an unresolved fact changes the recommendation, BLOCKED when no usable basis or safe fallback exists. The disposition grants no approval.

## MW-02 · Write a concept or treatment

**Take:** Audience change, controlling brief or notes, candidate story material, actual creative and production constraints.  
**Return:** One short concept with SUMMARY, STORY, VISUAL LOOK and ELEMENTS.  
**Pass:** A decision maker can picture the piece, explain how it helps the viewer, and choose a direction. A producer can name what needs obtaining or verifying.

1. Write the viewer premise: “After seeing [specific experience], [viewer] can [understand, feel or do something specific].” Pick the form that delivers it: observation, demonstration, interview, explanation, music-led or graphic. A demonstration needs clearly visible causality. A profile can use lived moments and an earned closing. A music-led montage carries its argument through sequencing, images and readable text.
2. Draft **SUMMARY** as one paragraph with viewer, idea, approach and payoff. Draft **STORY** as opening, development and ending, adding beats only as needed. For each beat state what happens, what it changes in the viewer’s understanding, and how the material supports it. Write one materially different option if uncertainty affects the decision.
3. In **VISUAL LOOK**, explain the choices that convey tone: distance, duration, natural sound, music, performance, graphic weight and visual legibility. Name what shifts for muted, large-screen, small-screen or translated access.
4. In **ELEMENTS**, write verified evidence beside its source, asset/people/access and rights status, decisive production constraint, approval or confirmation owner, and a lower-resource fallback. Keep uncertain service facts outside audience-facing language. Never fabricate participation or footage.

**Example (proposed).** **SUMMARY:** A hands-on demonstration lets an educator see why moving an object a little between photos makes it appear to move. **STORY:** Begin with the finished motion. Cut wide to the same object and a fixed camera, then show three changed positions. End by replaying those frames together. **VISUAL LOOK:** Uncluttered tabletop, stable overhead view, clear position markers and minimal sound that leaves room for a spoken explanation. **ELEMENTS:** Proposed tabletop capture, object and authorized location. Hold any grade/subject and direct resource invitation until the fact owner confirms the route. Fallback to an original graphic recreation if capture is unavailable. Decision requested: develop this demonstration or use an existing clip.

## MW-03 · Pitch a decision, design a series and write an episode

**Take:** Evidence of audience need, comparable work, route and rights facts, delivery capacity and scoped stakeholder authority.  
**Return:** One pitch with an explicit decision request or a series design with pilot, repeatable engine, sample slate and continuation decision.  
**Pass:** The reader can approve, revise, defer or decline a named commitment and justify that decision with audience value, feasibility and evidence.

1. Lead with one primary outcome, evidence, recommendation, lighter option and the ask. Put numbers, objections, limitations, cost or capacity consequence, date, and decision owner in the pre-read. Match the lead to its audience. A producer needs resource, risk and scheduling consequences. A marketing manager needs reachable audience and pathway. A communications director needs scope of the public claim, institutional reasoning and review route. An executive needs the effect, strategic fit, tradeoff and decision window.
2. For a series, establish the repeated audience problem and why one evergreen title is insufficient. Write a **promise**, **episode engine** (the repeatable viewer experience), **variable** (what changes each episode), required **proof** and **production ceiling**. Search the existing catalogue and compare real substitutes before commissioning new work. Check whether partners, participants, source evidence and approvals can support more than the pilot.
3. Write a sample slate as one complete unit per episode: question, working title, new proof/scene, audience payoff, next action, assets/rights, effort and alternate. For the pilot, settle entry episode, audience context, runnable test, known alternatives, release owner, capacity to repeat, evidence of result, and stop/revise/continue criteria.
4. Author the episode as one useful answer. Set the promise early, deliver it fully, repeat only the signature beats that help recognition, and write the next step so a new viewer can arrive independently. For a short-form cutdown, keep a single complete payoff. Shorten the idea and re-time media for the placement.
5. Record feedback by scope and owner. Capture which proposal the stakeholder chose and what was conditional. Revise the pilot and workback when a dispositive objection is supported. Treat funding, staffing, contributor contact and publication as separately authorized commitments.

**Copyable pitch pre-read:** Decision by [date] | Decision maker [name/scope] | [audience] needs [specific change] | evidence/limits [sources] | recommend [one route] | lighter option [alternative] | costs and risk [staff/time/approval] | pilot and test [what, who, when] | action if the decision is deferred [owner/next].  
**Illustrative episode engine:** “One overlooked research step, demonstrated with an original example, checked with a subject specialist, ending with a route the learner can try.” A possible pilot shows one precise search refinement. A second episode can answer how to narrow sources by format. Continue if service owners can sustain verification and observed viewer tests show the intended audience can repeat the step. Revise or pause if viewers follow the clip but cannot use the destination, or if per-episode verification and staffing exceed the agreed ceiling. Establish specific thresholds from scope.

## MW-04 · Author the audiovisual script

**Take:** Controlling brief and concept, audience and deliverable, target runtime, supplied assets, required evidence, source transcript where applicable.  
**Return:** A full editable section draft or a production table with Section, Audio, Video, source/asset notes and review disposition.  
**Pass:** A reader can perform the intended audio, an editor can imagine and assemble the exact beats with authorized material, and a reviewer can locate every consequential claim and change.

1. Pick a mode. For pre-shoot scripting, author spoken lines and real performance/visual instruction. For interview planning, write open prompts and answer targets. For paper editing, cite exact source wording, speaker, asset and a usable locator, marking omissions and authored bridges. Label approximate transcript locators; playback verifies the actual selected interval and context. Recheck any rearrangement that creates a different meaning.
2. Plan what the viewer sees, knows or feels at start, development and end. Compose audio and image together. The visual may prove the action while voice supplies orientation or causal context. Deliberate overlap supports comprehension when needed. Write exact narration, dialogue, pauses, music, effects and silence alongside exact action, camera/view, image source, on-screen words, graphics and transitions.
3. Write for breath and performance. Say the lines aloud, shorten noun clusters, mark pronunciation for verification, and preserve pauses and sound holds. Read the full sequence with physically feasible coverage. Identify each existing, proposed or unavailable visual; choose a fallback for essential proof. Sketch long/vertical variants when composition or audience use changes.
4. Time the draft. Use authored spoken words divided by a measured or clearly marked assumed words-per-minute rate, plus non-overlapping holds and transitions. Simulate actions and read the completed script aloud. Record whether values are estimates or measured, overlap explicitly, and locate what needs trimming. A stated finish time, displayed titles, and timings must agree.
5. Track access and review. Include understandable muted viewing where the placement requires it, plan captions and descriptive alternatives where applicable, and preview exact text and image against intended size and pacing. Record which sections depend on consent, source material, claims, rights or exact approvals. Bind caption timings to the accepted picture version. Recheck affected caption segments and derivatives after a trim. [W3C media accessibility guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/)

**Draft schema:** header = project, purpose, viewer, format/platform, target runtime, version, status and source basis. Each repeated section includes timing, complete spoken words, sound cues, pauses, primary action, supporting imagery, exact on-screen text, transition, access need, and source or proposed asset. Production format combines these groups in three columns. Use `None` for intentionally absent components and `TBD — owner: [role]` for consequential unknowns.

**Worked 30-second demonstration (proposed; timings are estimates and include holds).**

| Section | Audio | Video |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 · 0:00–0:05 · Outcome | VO: “This paper shape looks like it’s moving.” Hold a beat. | Show the completed original frame animation. Proposed tabletop asset V01. |
| 02 · 0:05–0:13 · Mechanism | VO: “Move it a little. Take a photo. Then do it again.” Three quiet shutter cues spaced after the corresponding motion. | Overhead: demonstrate three positions. Maintain stable camera and legible position markers. On-screen: “Move. Photograph. Repeat.” |
| 03 · 0:13–0:23 · Payoff | VO: “Play the photos in order, and those small moves become one motion.” Pause for playback. | Show three contact-sheet frames followed by playback. Source V01, to be captured. Keep captions clear of the moving shape. |
| 04 · 0:23–0:30 · Next step | VO: “Try it with a shape you can draw and cut out.” Hold on title. | End on shape and text “Try three small moves.” Grade, subject and any direct programme CTA remain held pending confirmation. |

**Failure switch:** If current approved direction is music-only and an old draft contains natural sound, bind the new draft to the current approval evidence, record what changed, and let images/text carry the meaning. If no matching visual exists, rewrite the beat or source lawful coverage. A renderer checks/exports authored values. Human reading and exact playback remain distinct acceptance checks.

## MW-05 · Adapt for audience, purpose and placement

**Take:** Approved or proposed core message, audience contexts, channel purpose, grounded facts and usable destination.  
**Return:** Versioned copy or script variants with distinct starts, evidence and decision/use scope.  
**Pass:** A reader gets their relevant fact and action without guesswork. Each placement’s opening pays off the packaging and its action can be completed in the receiving context.

1. Write what this audience knows, wants, worries about and can do now. Identify one credible proof and one action. Preserve essentials: programme name, participation conditions, effective dates, location and required access where verified. Build the object so later variants can inherit the same claims without importing unrelated copy.
2. Write to the use. **Web:** lead with task, eligibility and action or route, group detail under meaningful headings, and write descriptive links. **Educator email:** lead with learning value, audience fit, time/requirements and dates or access route. **Family social:** show one concrete benefit early, use short plain phrasing, and make the next step visible and accessible. **Short video:** script a first image or line that delivers a truthful expectation and offer one complete idea. **Long video:** align title and thumbnail with the early payoff and order context so each next section earns time. **Internal staff copy:** lead with the thing staff must know or do, effective time, responsibilities and escalation route.
3. Read aloud and preview each actual surface. Check everyday language, meaningful headings, qualifications, legibility, linked destination and accessibility. If a channel lacks a working route, redesign the copy or route the issue instead of promising a nonfunctional action. Communications checks claim/voice, Marketing checks audience and promise, Content checks the exact version and placement, Production checks timed on-screen/read-aloud delivery. Local authority confirms each use. [Canada.ca Content Style Guide](https://design.canada.ca/style-guide/)

**Example:** A family opening is “Make a paper shape move, one photo at a time.” An educator opening is “Use a sequence of photos to show how small changes create motion.” An internal production note is “Show the resulting motion first; verify audience scope before adding the link or grade label.” Shared proof: the same three-frame example. Keep programme invitation and applicability in a separate held field until confirmed.

## MW-06 · Write executive, host and live spoken scripts

**Take:** Named speaker, tone/use, reviewed facts, exact time and run-of-show dependencies, audience needs and approval scope.  
**Return:** Performable speaking copy, optional timing and cue sheet, current review authority and exact holder of any proviso.  
**Pass:** The speaker can rehearse it aloud, the operator knows what each cue triggers, and the reviewer knows which version and excerpt are approved.

1. Write the first useful line, the one main thought, then the closing action or feeling. For an executive, write the people-facing consequence, what the speaker can responsibly say and who owns next steps. Hold prediction or authority claims that the institutional owner has not cleared. For a host, write names and transitions as spoken, introductions with verified preferences, a reset line for a delay, and how to hand back. Separate operator cues from spoken words.
2. Read aloud, verify names/pronunciation with an authorized source, state timing evidence, and offer an easier alternate line when a tongue-twister or sensitive term changes performance. Link every imperative cue to the run of show or name an operator and fallback. Do not improvise incident messaging in the rehearsal packet.
3. Use **Cue/time | Speaker and exact line | Operator action | Condition/fallback | Reviewer**. Mark portions restricted to an approved audience. If a section is held, provide independent ready sections only under the allowed use. Name the decision on any provisional host handoff and the separately scoped final authorization.

## MW-07 · Revise and issue writing for review

**Take:** Exact original draft, current feedback, scope and approval record, recipients and linked artefacts.  
**Return:** Exact revised draft plus concise change and impact note.  
**Pass:** A reviewer finds their changes, the relevant authority owns any conflicts, and recipients can tell which version they may use.

1. Tag feedback required change, suggestion, question or approval. Bind to reviewer, exact text/section/time and intended decision. Route disagreement to the named decision owner. Keep approved facts distinct from unapproved direction.
2. Change only affected content. Recheck timing, evidence/quotes, captions, graphics, derivatives, destinations and uses where the basis moved. Never transfer approval to an altered version by inference.
3. Send or stage within stated authority. Return **Draft/version | changes | unaffected basis | affected linked versions | holds, owner and date | requested decision | recipient and access/receipt check**. A marked draft can travel only within its exact approved scope; final-required use waits for authorized delivery and receipt of the final.

## Repeatable workflows

| Workflow | Sequence and decision branches | Finished result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Intake to approved writing basis** | MW-01 → owner confirms or corrects affected facts → MW-02 or MW-03 if creative/capacity decisions remain → record decision. If the issue is access or capacity, route repair before promotion. | Referenceable approved scope or separately marked branch and next owner. |
| **Confirm, write, release** | MW-04 → read-through and receiver review → MW-07 for changes → MW-05 packaging → production and release checks for exact asset/version/destination. A trim reopens dependent work, and a destination failure holds affected invitation while independent work continues. | Exact working script and bounded copy; release depends on its own checks. |
| **Series pilot to renewal** | MW-03 defines hypothesis and ceiling → MW-04 writes pilot → MW-05 tests audience paths → record cost, clarity and audience outcome → decide continue, revise or pause. | Pilot, episode map, evidence and next decision with scope. |

## Worked brief · Three small moves

**Illustrative planning copy.** **Decision needed:** Confirm the educator resource path before commissioning promotion. The proposed output is an original 30-second demonstration showing how one movement becomes a sequence. Earliest external use: TBD — owner: campaign coordinator.

### 1. Project Context

**Background:** Educators can reach the resource through a public hub and a detail page. Prior practice inquiry identified different grade and subject descriptions. Verify the current versions and controlling description with the programme owner. **Opportunity:** Help educators judge whether the activity fits their classes. A clear page may solve the primary issue by itself.

### 2. Goal & Objectives

**Primary Goal:** An interested educator can assess the activity's purpose and find the correct resource. **Objectives:** Verify both destinations, compare a page fix and an original demo, and test comprehension before spending on variants. **Mandatory Content Considerations:** Withhold grade, subject and programme CTA pending confirmation. Proposed strategic link: Experiences through a tangible activity educators can bring to learners.

### 3. Tone & Audience

**Audience Profile:** Educators scanning for a classroom activity without prior animation experience. **Tone Keywords:** Practical, curious and concise. **Visual Reference / Moodboard:** A stable overhead tabletop view with clear stages. **Key Message:** Small changes between photos produce visible motion.

### 4. Deliverables & Technical Specs

| Asset Type | Ratio | Runtime | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main Feature | Proposed 16:9 | 30 s, estimate | Demonstrate one activity if the route needs media |
| Accessibility Assets | Match accepted cut | Match accepted cut | Provide captions and any needed descriptive text |

### 5. How This Video Will Be Used

**Distribution Channels:** Consider the verified resource page first. **Context of Viewing:** Desktop or mobile during lesson planning. Verify muted comprehension and exact destination in the intended context.

### 6. Talent Summary & Production Specs

**Talent Requirements:** Proposed hands and voice, no featured patron required. **Locations:** Authorized tabletop workspace, TBD — owner: producer. **Visual Style:** Instructional close detail and original materials.

### 7. Timeline & Stakeholder Milestones

| Milestone | Target Date | Role | Stakeholder |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Confirm programme fact | Before external copy | Fact owner | Programme lead to confirm |
| Commission, repair or reuse decision | After route check | Decision maker | Named project owner |
| First cut and review | After production commitment | Editor and reviewers | Assigned production and programme roles |
| First audience access | After exact version and link clearance | Release owner | Authorized publisher |

**Disposition: RESEARCH FIRST.** Route the current page comparison and confirm whether a video addresses the remaining viewer need.

**Practice basis.** This portable manual reconstructs methods from the existing brief, video-concept and A/V writing procedures, the pre-production and production/post-production playbooks and the work-practice inquiry. Historical inquiry supports three cautions: milestone labels may conflate distinct cuts and handoffs, positive internal feedback may precede scoped approval, and archived drafts may conflict with controlling creative direction. Treat these as check patterns. The examples here are authored proposals and do not assert local permission, approved messaging, delivery or measured audience effects.
