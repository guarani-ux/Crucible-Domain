# Media production practice

**Use for** production decisions, camera and sound preparation, shoots, stills, livestreams, edit and delivery. **Provide** the current brief and A/V script or event intent, real dates, assets, restrictions, environment and recipients. **Return** the requested editable packet plus checks, owners and open decisions. Start with the row that yields the deliverable. A role card assigns the work. The procedures below supply the method.

| If the assignment is… | Use | Receiver |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choose a feasible form or plan competing deadlines | MP-01 treatment/workback | Human decision owner or production manager |
| Prepare camera coverage, staffing, or on-set directions | MP-02 shot list; MP-03 setup; MP-04 call sheet; MP-05 direction and permissions | Crew, participant owner and producer |
| Capture reusable material or deliver photographs and isolated sound | MP-06 capture and shared shoot | Editor, producer and each named outlet |
| Plan a remote or hybrid event | MP-07 live run-of-show and rehearsal | Platform operator, school-side observer and event owner |
| Receive a shoot or recover an unavailable file | MP-08 wrap and ingest | Editor, storage custodian and rights owner when needed |
| Turn interviews and footage into a story | MP-09 selects and paper edit | Story editor and creative decision owner |
| Finish, review, caption or pass to publication | MP-10 cut/QC; MP-11 release package and practice loop | Reviewer, audience-side verifier and publication owner |
| Prepare a kit or route a failure | MP-12 equipment stewardship and substitute | Next operator and equipment custodian |

**Working boundary.** PROD-D makes a treatment recommendation, PROD-M coordinates the draft workback and dependencies, and PROD-S drafts or performs qualified digital operations. Use reviewable states such as proposed, confirmed, reviewed, accepted and superseded. Physical capture, on-air changes, group invitations, equipment commitments, consent, card disposal and publication go to the people with actual authority. Asana remains read-only. Keep restricted files and identifying details in their authorised systems.

## MP-01 · Choose a treatment and plan the workback

**Take** the audience promise, intended action or experience, delivery versus first-use dates, approved direction, existing material, crew and review capacity. **Return** one production decision or versioned workback. **Accept when** a decision owner can distinguish a feasible commitment, unresolved decision and the next task.

1. Write the promise in one sentence. Identify the first viewer action and the proof needed to support it. Check assets before claiming they exist and distinguish available, playable, and permitted. Compare existing material, simple demonstration, interview-led video, photo or motion treatment, or live only where each would serve the purpose. For each candidate say what it proves, work it costs, and what could fail.
2. Specify the anchor format and derivatives. Identify first external use separately from public launch, programme and creative approval separately from release. Work backward from the controlling milestone through delivery, playback/QC, caption or access checks, lock, review turnaround, edit blocks, checked ingest, capture, and access gates. Use a calendar for working days, timezone and fixed commitments. Label estimates as proposals.
3. Identify each real prerequisite and independent thread. A tentative room or participant holds the affected shoot, but file checks, graphic planning and parallel brief work can proceed. Estimate prep, setup, capture, transfer, edit, waiting and verification separately. Do not double-book an operator during simultaneous cues or a reviewer during an unavailable window. Compare the remaining capacity with other due work.
4. If timing will not fit, return options with impact: reduce a secondary variant, sequence approved partial handoff, replan team effort through the human owner, or seek a new date. Preserve fixed dates and approvals until the owner changes them. A gained shoot block can still incur downstream editing or caption work.

Read the parent task, subtasks and controlling comments separately in the authorised project. Map dates by the artifact and receiving action each actually governs. If a record labelled first cut carries the final-export date and a named subtask describes an earlier cut, preserve both observations and resolve first cut, review and actual export as separate milestones through the coordinator. For a stills request that also mentions video, record the possible second artifact separately. Confirm its creative intent, owner and capacity before promising it. A photo receipt supplies no evidence of video completion.

**Copyable workback columns:** Task ID | verb and concrete output | maker | approver/receiver | prerequisite IDs | effort block | due and timezone | fixed/calculated/proposed | readiness and hold | receiving check. Example: `Q-03 · Check phone playback of V02 and caption sidecar · accessibility reviewer · requires locked V02 and exact sidecar · due Friday noon local · fixed release gate · report names/timing and overlap against exact versions.` The user may enter reviewed tasks manually.

## MP-02 · Write the shot list a crew can use

**Take** current A/V beats, location, known actions, permitted participants, format and time. **Return** a setup-sorted shot list and protected coverage order. **Accept when** each essential beat has a visible or audible success check and an owner for gaps.

1. Convert each script beat to an observable action or sound. Plan the smallest complete set: an orienting view when place matters, a whole action the editor can reconstruct, a readable detail for the decisive step, and an expression or clean finish when it carries meaning. Add B-roll for evidence, orientation or a justified bridge. Verify dates, signage, context and whether a person or background can be shown. An unverified photo supports neither a promised video shot nor an identifiable user's story.
2. Group by location, participant, camera/light/sound setup and continuity. Mark essential, supporting or optional explicitly. Add an alternate only for a named risk such as no screen clearance or a clipped action when cropped. Test dedicated portrait framing if a horizontal crop loses hand movement or crucial space.
3. Make the returned capture log use the same IDs. Log captured, verified alternate, present but unverified, held, or pickup with actual recording IDs. Mark owner and due date for each missing essential. Have the editor trace all essential beats to exact rows.

| Shot / beat | Setup and action | Sound / orientation | Priority and minimum usable take | Approved use or hold | Alternative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S03 / demonstrate first step | Slight overhead on tabletop, hands complete one action from rest to result | Record clean cue and meaningful action sound; capture dedicated portrait if hands leave crop | Essential; focus holds through movement, both start and result visible | Verify visible display and hands with producer | Cleared rehearsal interface or approved illustration, labelled as such |

## MP-03 · Choose and prove the camera, sound and light setup

**Take** planned shots, actual space, verified available kit, distribution and ingest constraints. **Return** a setup card and a request for sample evidence. **Accept when** the actual recorded path yields a representative sample the receiver can open and inspect.

| Story constraint | Start here and test | If it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interview trust and focus | Put camera near the intended eye line. Try medium framing with purposeful background separation. Start from a window or bounced soft key plus controllable fill if available. Monitor the recorded speech path as the subject looks or turns. Check focus, exposure and background with a recorded sample. | Reposition camera or chair if the eye line distracts; simplify background or move to quieter space. Compare a properly positioned boom or lavalier where authorised. Fix clothing noise or off-axis intelligibility before the main take. |
| Movement or detailed demonstration | Pick camera position according to visible action, then choose a focal length that supports it. Verify focus across the actual motion. Use a wide context take, side or overhead process view, and a close detail if material. Test readability of demonstration screens and essential action in the intended crop. | Keep an uninterrupted whole action before adding detail. Use an approved prop demo, cleared graphics or qualified pick-up if physical access or readability is missing. |
| Mixed sources, stage or public space | Inspect test files and source metadata to confirm frame rate, colour profile and audio format before selecting matching and finishing settings. Listen for speech and ambient interruption; test reflections and flicker at the recorded settings. Inspect background identity and confidential surfaces. | Adjust only verified gear/settings within crew authority, and log the changed profile. Get audience or audio-side readback if live. Hold capture, reposition or use cleared illustrative material if privacy, safety or access is unresolved. |

**Copyable setup card:** Setup ID | protected action and view | available gear and verified location | profile/frame rate/audio tracks | key and environment | microphone and monitoring route | focus/exposure/motion check | test file/attendee receipt | alternate | blocking decision. Set actual exposure, output profile and audio targets from evidence and the approved destination. Read the file and watch/listen. A metadata probe identifies properties, while an editor or observer confirms whether the action and meaning survive. Never treat untested described equipment as available.

## MP-04 · Write and check the call sheet

**Take** accepted workback, shoot timezone and location, real roles, windows, access and scope, shot list and contingency. **Return** one current call sheet with role-specific views. **Accept when** each person can state when and where they are needed, their first job, who decides a change and how the wrap handoff works.

Top line: **Project / date and timezone / version / status / location access route / first critical cue / producer contact route / unresolved gate.** Below it list arrival, load-in and setup, participant windows, test, protected capture blocks, breaks, reset, wrap and editor handoff. Attach only role-appropriate contact and access details using authorised channels. Mark proposed windows and missing approvals. Identify early decision cutoff for weather, venue access, equipment substitute, crew or participant changes. Use actual site safety procedures and name the human stop authority. A single person needs sequential tasks for work that requires attention simultaneously. Get a read-back from the responsible human rather than treating a send as acceptance. Issue a dated delta if call time, location, audience use or protected coverage changes and reconcile who got each version.

**Copyable timeline:** Local time | minutes | ready gate | who/where | setup/shot/cue IDs | must leave with | fallback + decision owner. Example: `09:25–09:40 · test · location approved and audio path assigned · camera/sound · S01–04 · actual recorded playback verified by editor/crew · switch to approved quieter location if intelligibility fails.`

## MP-05 · Direct interviews and route rights respectfully

**Take** contributor brief, agreed topic and use scope, quotation/fact owners, and coverage. **Return** a short director card and post-interview notes. **Accept when** a supported soundbite can be recovered in context and every restricted use has a human owner.

1. Before directing, confirm pronunciation, agreed topics, practical access and who verifies permission for capture, internal review and intended publication. Keep participation voluntary, consent records restricted and special access needs private. Route questions involving children, incidental bystanders, sensitive context, cultural review, proprietary screens or music to the appropriate authorised owner. Make the uncertainty visible. Record “permission unverified” instead of assuming silence or prior publication grants reuse.
2. Start with an open description, follow with a specific example or demonstration, then ask what varies and what the audience should do. If someone uses specialist terms, request their plain-language explanation. Ask for a self-contained restatement in their words when an answer relies on the unheard question. Do not supply the quotation. Redirect an out-of-scope topic or stop recording through the human director where needed.
3. Record clip/time, surrounding meaning, exact fact to check, source owner, alternate and usage limitation. Ask for clarification before accepting a statement whose shortened form would promise more than the speaker meant.

**Card row:** Beat | question | clue to follow up | desired proof/capture | fact/rights owner | locator/status. Example: `Get help with a first step · What usually happens when a first-time visitor arrives? · If they say “today,” ask what depends on availability · capture exact next step; verify through programme owner · Q2 / source time.`

## MP-06 · Plan a shared shoot and discrete photo or audio outputs

**Take** anchor assignment, candidate uses, allowed scopes and protected contributor windows. **Return** one capture matrix and per-destination output checklist. **Accept when** each actual file can be traced to its intended allowed use and essentials survive a shorter session.

1. Start from the anchor beat and shoot its complete action or speech before variations. Attach each additional episode or use at shot level. Account for added takes, label/track work, clearance and review. If behind, drop optional derivatives before protected shared essentials. Never transfer authorisation from one destination to another.
2. For photography, build a chooseable set covering establishing context, representative action, and purposeful details. Record identity where authorised, creation date, credits, suitable crops, caption/alt-text handoff and specific usage rights. Check the proposed crop and whether the picture supports the accompanying claim.
3. For an audio-only or isolated sound job, specify speech, meaningful sound and room tone, separation of sources where available, target use and technical spec owner. Preserve clean intelligibility and recording continuity. Deliver exact master and needed tracks, captions/transcript or accessibility owner, credits, rights and playback receipt. Demo sound and voice must survive the audience listening conditions where relevant.

**Coverage matrix:** unit ID | anchor beat | secondary placement | added time and format | person/context restrictions | file/version | cutoff priority | status. **Kit exception:** equipment ID | observed fault | unavailable use | tested substitute evidence | owner for service | downstream schedule impact. Human crews confirm kit, setup and venue safety before use.

## MP-07 · Write the live run of show and qualify the route

**Take** audience and event goal, programme timing, actual platform mode, role assignment, attendee path, rehearsal slot and decision owner. **Return** a live packet, rehearsal log and scoped go/no-go recommendation. **Accept when** host controls and attendee playback pass from the intended sides, and a named owner can explain the tested fallback.

1. Outline: preflight and join, hold/standby, welcome, segment transitions, participant contribution and chat or Q&A management, break, close, stop and what happens to the recording. For each cue give caller, operator, action, source or feed, concurrent job, expected attendee result and fallback. Put operations that overlap on different people or explicitly simplify the show.
2. Trace mic/cameras/slides and any room mixer to capture, platform and participant output. Keep roles distinct: event owner authorises decisions, platform operator confirms control, presenter confirms content and access, and observer checks the actual attendee view. Test voice, slides or demo, movement, on-screen text/captions and programme links using the real configuration and representative device. If present, separately inspect recording completion and playback. Record evidence by cue and observer.
3. **Teams branch.** Establish whether the job is a meeting, webinar or town hall and verify the actual controls and role assignments. For a town hall, Microsoft documents production-tool ownership options in event settings. Have the assigned person demonstrate the needed operations in rehearsal. A presenter who joins does not establish host control or viewer playback. **Zoom branch.** Verify actual mode and account setup. Webinar practice involves named host/panelist participants and a Webinar entitlement under Zoom's documented requirements. Check the attendee view separately in an authorised rehearsal condition. [Teams town hall controls](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/meetings/control-town-hall-production-tools-in-microsoft-teams) · [Zoom Webinar practice](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0062566)
4. Test a plausible loss of feed, unavailable presenter or echo response. Name who makes go/no-go and when, how viewers are told, and the tested route to pause, use a reduced feed, insert an authorised stand-in or postpone. Keep live stand-in and post-event replay as independent usage decisions. A stand-in needs an exact asset, intended audience approval, rights, accessibility and tested cue insertion/recovery. Replay needs its own permitted use, exact version, expiry or access, and audience playback. Untested Zoom, playback or recording remains a proposal or hold.

For a school event, identify the controlling show flow, crew call sheet, audience and host URLs, room, deck or demo file, audience accessibility and recording route. Keep the recording edit, content review and archive acceptance on separate job rows. Success of the live event cannot establish the archive is usable.

**Cue row:** local time/cue | caller, operator and observer | source/meaning | operator acknowledgement | audience-side evidence | failure trigger, fallback and authoriser | actual recording. Example: `C07 · show hands-on demo · presenter cues, switcher takes overhead, observer confirms instruction and visible control · if feed fails, hold with approved neutral slate and live guidance until owner calls recovery.`

## MP-08 · Wrap, copy, sync and verify ingest

**Take** actual camera/audio originals, capture log, permission scopes, storage destinations and editor. **Return** versioned source/permission and transfer manifest. **Accept when** editor receipt and useful media are verified separately from wrap and copy.

1. Match shot IDs to actual sources, card/recorder and paths. Preserve originals and card structure where required. Authorised human operators transfer to approved storage and record source/destination counts and integrity evidence. Keep a held clip and its derivatives within permitted storage and access. The current production practice calls for two verified copies before card clearing, with disposal separately authorised. A failed source verification holds affected reuse and clearing. Retain the source, diagnose and recheck any authorised retry.
2. Inspect frame/audio metadata, open a representative clip, verify varied source cases and log errors. Sync with slate, matching sound or available verified timecode. Watch lip sync and distant points for drift, especially on long captures or separately recorded audio. Record what was used, offset or unresolved discrepancy, and verifier. Provide original/proxy mapping and have the editor test relink plus the original used for export. Adobe documents the reconnect operation for compatible project clips. [Premiere proxy reconnect](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/organize-media/ingest-proxy-workflow/reconnect-full-resolution-media-to-proxies.html)
3. Deliver exact manifest revision and destination. Ask editor to confirm access, playback/sync/relink and usable scope. Return a separate pickup or alternative for missing media. Preserve confirmed copy, editor receipt and verified editorial usability as independent states.

**Row:** Original/card ID | shot/beat | path | checked copy + evidence | audio/source offset | proxy map + original check | authorised use | verified by/date | pickup or resolution owner. Optional authorised read-only inspection if installed: `ffprobe -v error -show_format -show_streams -of json "$input_file"`. This reads technical properties, not permissions or viewer experience. [ffprobe documentation](https://ffmpeg.org/ffprobe.html)

## MP-09 · Pull documentary selects and make a paper edit

**Take** synced accessible sources, confirmed treatment, source permissions, transcript if available and intended receiver. **Return** source-linked selects, paper edit or stringout plus gaps. **Accept when** editor can locate each consequential quote or visual, identify the context and see why it supports the beat.

1. Seek likely moments using transcript/search or a stringout, then listen/watch original surrounding material. Verify actual words, names, inclusive context, source position and timebase. Keep qualifications. Where source is unavailable, report a candidate and required check rather than claiming exact speech.
2. Label essential proof, supporting context, optional alternative and missing proof. Choose paper edit when multiple contributors or critical structure require inexpensive review. Use a stringout for comparing delivery or visual options. Start directly in timeline when the few verified beats are straightforward.
3. Sequence what the viewer understands and when. Set promise, orientation, proof, implication and next action. Read or watch through, timing naturally. If testimony makes a claim that visuals cannot support, choose an authorised pickup, clearer context or narrower authorised wording instead of inventing B-roll or juxtaposing misleading footage.

**Select row:** clip/source in–out + timebase | actual quote/observed action | context/qualifier | budgeted duration | beat/use | rights/status | alternate/gap. **Paper edit row:** story order | audience question | verified source refs | audio and visual relationship | runtime estimate | check/owner. Example: if “today, if there is room” qualifies an offer, carry the whole condition or route a separate fact before promising availability.

## MP-10 · Edit, caption and QC the exact cut

**Take** authorised media and scope, exact timeline, audience promise, destination specs and reviewer. **Return** a versioned cut, captions/access plan and timed QC/review record. **Accept when** the actual rendition holds evidence and intelligibility, and named reviewer can act on exact version and decision scope.

1. **Rough:** protect structure before polish. Check each essential beat against source and permission. Mark temps, missing pickups, unconfirmed names and rights holds in the review packet. Ask a fresh viewer what they understood before explaining. Check point of view, promises and what the cut implies.
2. **Fine:** refine pauses, action continuity, the role of ambient sound and music, and coherence of available colour profiles. Listen for voice intelligibility, sync, clips/noise and unwanted transitions, using realistic speakers/headphones. Inspect the densest overlap of voice, motion, text and captions. If viewers miss the message, simplify text, adjust edit timing or remove competing elements. Confirm source history and rights before using stock, music or graphics.
3. **Access:** plan from the brief. On the exact picture version, check speech and meaningful sounds, names, speaker clarity where needed, timing, line breaks and overlap with essential visual information. Check a transcript or audio description route when required. Caption generation yields a draft, playback/QC yields observed evidence. Review the actual destination and crops when available. [W3C captions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/captions/)
4. Record required change vs suggestion vs question, by time/asset, owner, exact version and disposition. One timeline change can affect captions, music, timed graphics, crops and derivative approvals. A description-only change may affect wording, link and recipient authorisation while leaving unchanged picture-related checks intact. List affected checks, confirm lock explicitly, and reconcile copies.

**QC row:** cut ID + timestamp | expected/observed | evidence and surface | severity | owner | resulting version | recheck and reviewer. Example: `V03 00:18–00:21 · lower-third overlaps timed caption on portrait view · recorded playback · caption timing/placement owner · verify V04 caption and readable claim before release.`

## MP-11 · Package, pass to publication and learn

**Take** reviewed exact versions, specifications, rights, caption and credit info, destinations and first external use. **Return** manifest and a receiving check for each asset and outlet. **Accept when** the correct recipient can open the exact version and correctly identify what they may do next.

1. Package each master and derivative, caption/transcript, thumbnail or still if assigned, approved copy or credit, scope and known exclusions. Review exported files for opening, key transitions, ending, duration, track/caption behaviour and intended crop. Verify audience-facing link, eligibility or registration path with the appropriate owner. Mark uploader, release and actual audience checks as distinct delegated work.
2. State named reviewer, publisher, programme owner, due date and remaining hard gates. For a scoped draft, mark the version, supported audience and exclusions. Keep final-required use held pending authorised final and recipient receipt. Exact first exposure and amplification can have different dates. Verify accessible receiving, acknowledgement and audience-side experience where permitted.
3. After work, record one failure or successful adjustment tied to evidence and the next variable to change. E.g., a Q&A operator missed feed audio while switching. Reassign one role or simplify the cue, and compare the next audience-side rehearsal. If reviewers could not separate sound and call-to-action text, run a phone comprehension check on the densest scene before revision cost rises. Log retention and affected workflow, actual result and limits.

For an audio announcement sent to multiple locations, record cue or chime approval separately from speech intelligibility. Ask representative receiving sites to check actual playback in context, route device or vendor level changes to the system owner, and record sites where the test or recovery has yet to happen. A clean master or cue approval does not verify installed playback.

**Package manifest row:** deliverable/version | outlet/recipient | media + companion files | check/date/evidence | approved use or hold | first permitted audience use | next action and owner | access/receipt/public verification.

## MP-12 · Check equipment readiness and qualify a substitute

**Take** the approved coverage plan, kit list, actual room or field conditions and the custodian role. **Return** one availability and substitution record. **Accept when** the next operator can identify what they may take, which signal path passed its check, and who owns the remaining failure.

1. Map each essential signal to the actual device, connection and receiving path. Mark what must keep working together. Include media and power, mic-to-recorder, isolation and monitoring, platform or recorder feed, mounts, and any physical constraints. Record model, approved configuration and ownership where known. A kit in inventory is a candidate until availability and condition are confirmed.
2. Ask the authorized operator to check setup behaviour and record a short representative trial before relying on a device. Verify recorded output or receiving route, with time, settings and evidence. Check what changed since last use, including labels, adapters, preset, storage and shared bookings. If repair is needed, capture observable symptoms and route to the custodian or vendor. Use authorized operators for configuration and equipment decisions.
3. If the planned device is unavailable, compare restore, substitute and reduce scope. Explain which function would be lost or changed and which checks the substitute invalidates. Rehearse or capture a sample with the substitute, record what passed, and update the setup and workback only when the project owner accepts the change. Preserve the original fault record.
4. At return, record what came back, known issues, confirms or holds, and the next custodian. Separate equipment availability, booked use, playback verified and equipment returned.

**Kit row:** function | identified device | availability owner/date | test and evidence | disposition | substitute and changed dependencies | next owner.

**Example.** If a camera supplying interview sound is replaced, the producer adds a check for the edited sound route, not just the replacement image. If the sound test fails, keep capture on hold until the authorized operator verifies a simpler route and the receiver can open it.
