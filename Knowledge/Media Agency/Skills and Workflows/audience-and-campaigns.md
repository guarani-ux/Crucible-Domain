# Audience research, marketing and campaign execution

Use any numbered practice directly with the assignment and its sources. Each produces an artifact for a named receiver. An agent may inspect authorized records, compare evidence, prepare drafts and propose checks. Asana remains read-only. Use public or authorized read-only research. The service owner decides offer facts and capacity, the Marketing Manager coordinates assignments, and each authorized sender or platform owner controls their own release. Check the current project for delegation.

## MK-01 · Find a content opportunity

**Skill. Input** · a request, affected audience, current service page and one decision date. **Return** · one-page opportunity note with a preferred intervention and lighter option. **Check** · the manager can see what audience obstacle each option addresses, who can remove it, and which fact could change the recommendation.

1. Translate the request to the action or understanding desired. Ask what people do today and where the journey breaks. Mark unobserved answers as assumptions.
2. Search the Library website and approved content inventory for the exact service, audience-language synonyms and common questions. Starting searches: `site:calgarylibrary.ca "Tech Mentors"`, then `site:calgarylibrary.ca "technology help"`. Inspect applicable details and exact destination pages. Check the channel catalogue or authorized inventory for a current answer, potentially reusable assets and duplication. Record search scope and gaps.
3. Walk the ordinary path from the audience's starting context to its next action. Check eligibility, date, access, accessibility, availability and whether the page explains what to expect. Route consent, permissions, service capacity and promotion decisions to their owners. If real audience evidence is missing, propose one observable staff read-back or bounded user research check.
4. Compare options in this order when feasible: fix a wrong/blocked destination, surface an existing resource, repackage or add a companion, commission new content. Estimate production, review and ongoing maintenance separately. Select one option conditional on the controlling evidence. Set a review/expiry owner for information likely to change.

**Copyable row** · Audience and current behaviour | barrier and source | intended action | existing asset/destination | option and effort | benefit and risk | owner and needed decision | what would reverse preference.

**Example.** An educator promotion proposes a new stop-motion video. The Educators index and the resource page display different grade and subject descriptions, and the resource page shows multiple existing videos and activities. Hold the affected promise. Ask the programme owner to confirm intended audience and scope using both URLs. An asset audit and neutral concept can run in parallel. If the destination is corrected and existing materials answer educators' demonstrated question, test discovery before new capture. [Educators](https://www.calgarylibrary.ca/connect/educators) · [Stop Motion resource](https://www.calgarylibrary.ca/connect/educators/stop-motion)

## MK-02 · Research claims, Board records and strategic fit

**Skill. Input** · exact proposed claim, brief or decision and intended audience. **Return** · dated source and confirmation table plus one sentence connecting an audience outcome to the applicable plan. **Check** · the audience and approver can distinguish public fact, approved decision, current operating condition and proposal.

1. Split compound claims into exact facts. For a current service condition use the controlling page and assigned service owner. For Board direction, find the meeting materials and approved minutes. Search `site:calgarylibrary.ca "approved minutes" "Tech Mentors"` and follow the official meeting index when the public website search misses. Read context, original attachments where available, the applicable motion/disposition, date and page. Use the current plan for a strategic claim.
2. Mark each item as current public information, historical reported finding, Board discussion/recommendation, approved decision, project proposal, or unresolved. Record the actual scope, source date and period of validity. Treat two documents repeating the same origin as one source. A draft agenda with a proposed motion cannot establish adoption.
3. For conflicting sources, record both wordings and date, the affected language/action, and a focused confirmation question. If the factual owner is unknown, draft an internal routing question for the project lead. Prepare a neutral approved-scope alternative while the owner confirms. A corrected fact does not clear public wording or release.
4. Use this fit sentence: “Helping [audience] do [action] advances [applicable mandate] because [explicit audience benefit].” Verify whether the project sponsor wants that strategic link externally or only internally. The 2027–2030 public plan names Expertise, Spaces, Collections, Technology and Experiences. Cite the relevant mandate, plan year and intended benefit. Keep the plan's future vision separate from observed performance. [Strategic Plan](https://www.calgarylibrary.ca/about-the-library/strategic-plan)

**Copyable row** · Claim ID and exact wording | source link/page/meeting date | status and effective scope | owner and date checked | conflict or qualifier | permitted usage | follow-up.

**Example.** A historic Board record describing tech help demand can support investigating a navigation problem. It doesn't establish current programme capacity. Check with the service contact. Proposed internal rationale: “Helping first-time visitors find the current way to get help ties a Technology placement to practical access.” Current options still need verification. [Meeting index](https://www.calgarylibrary.ca/about-the-library/library-board/meeting-information) · [March 2025 approved minutes](https://www.calgarylibrary.ca/assets/2025-03-19-Board-Minutes-approved.pdf) · [Tech Mentors](https://www.calgarylibrary.ca/events-and-programs/programs/tech-mentors)

## MK-03 · Scan peers and trends for a testable creative choice

**Skill. Input** · an audience question and creative or channel decision. **Return** · bounded peer/trend scan with one proposed adaptation, one counterexample and reproducible scope. **Check** · a peer can repeat the search and tell which creative choice the evidence supports.

1. Define the match first: audience intent, service category, format, channel, asset age and access context. Compare at least two comparable examples and one example intended to challenge the mechanism for a normal internal recommendation. Log a narrower sample if the corpus is small. Search the owned catalogue first. Use queries such as `site:youtube.com library "how to use" digital help`, `site:tpl.ca digital help library` and then named peers' search tools. Record exact queries, window and geography.
2. Open each actual asset and its destination when available. Record first frame or sentence, promise, proof/demo, narrative transition, call to action, duration and receiving conditions. Distinguish the actual behaviour shown from a hypothesis about why an audience responded. Compare date and distribution context before using view counts as a lead. Note what is unknowable from public metrics.
3. For search interest, enter comparable topics/terms together, confirm geography, date and search type, and save the settings. Try synonyms and a longer window to detect seasonality or a one-off spike. Record changed results. Google Trends shows relative normalized search interest and uses a sample, so compare within the same query context. Low signal calls for staff questions, catalogue or other appropriate evidence. [Google Trends FAQ](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533?hl=en-ca)
4. Describe the mechanism to test in local conditions. Choose one variable such as opening around a practical task, plain-language search phrasing or a demonstration before branding. Check format volatility, brand fit, rights and access before proposing imitation.

**Copyable row** · ID and URL | audience/problem/channel | observed timecode and mechanism | date and observable distribution | comparability/confound | plausible transfer | evidence that would change decision.

**Illustrative return.** Several accessible examples show a step before introducing the organization. A less comparable story-led piece shows a similar opening without a service action. Test the identity-plus-demonstration opening on the specific service asset with a comprehension check. Record the intended learning and evaluate it before adding it to a template.

## MK-04 · Research audience needs and test engagement

**Skill. Input** · target audience, intended action, draft variants and actual destination. **Return** · one audience hypothesis and one bounded experiment with an outcome, measurement plan and decision rule. **Check** · the test distinguishes weak discovery, unclear content, and a dead end.

1. Describe the audience by situation and job: “educator choosing a classroom activity” or “first-time visitor looking for help before a branch visit.” Check current public documentation, authorized audience questions, staff observations and actual access constraints. Avoid using an unverified demographic or collecting unnecessary personal details. Route direct participant research through authorized procedures. [Discovery research](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/user-research-in-discovery)
2. Write one hypothesis: “[audience] will [understand/do X] because [treatment addresses barrier Y].” Draw a chain of discover → choose → understand → act. Name the observation at each handoff. If the service has no current capacity, route the action/fallback first.
3. Set one primary measure matched to the weakest stage. For comprehension, ask a consenting representative viewer to explain the next step after seeing the draft. For discovery, use comparable click or view-entry evidence with defined denominators. For completing a task, inspect the real path or use authorized aggregate completion data. For returning to a series, define comparable window and audience cohort or clearly state the limitations.
4. Compare two accurate draft treatments that differ materially on one element. Keep placement, window, audience and destination as comparable as feasible. If comparison can't be isolated, report this as observed variation rather than causal evidence. Pick an interpretation rule before the test. Keep a quality/accessibility guardrail independent of gains.
5. Decide revise, repeat, scale or stop. Record a conflicting observation. Route any platform test, participant recruitment or external action to the authorized owner.

**Experiment card** · Audience/situation | obstacle | hypothesis | exact treatment A/B or baseline | comparable window | primary measure/denominator | guardrail | decision rule/owner | limitations.

**Illustrative treatments.** Educator opening A: “Teach sequence by making one frame at a time.” Opening B: “See what one classroom can create with stop motion.” Ask each participant to identify the activity and where they would find instructions. An evocative line that hides the next step warrants revision.

## MK-05 · Discover and shortlist partners

**Workflow. Input** · purpose, audience overlap needed, geography, desired contribution, timing and any restrictions. **Return** · a source-backed shortlist and decision to progress, clarify or pause. **Check** · the manager can tell who adds distinct public value, which promises are unverified and who can authorize contact.

1. Define partner job. Separate specialist knowledge, community trust, access to an audience, venue, programme or production contribution. Write minimum requirements and conflicts to check upfront. Consider representation, service capacity and whether a partnership would improve access.
2. Start with authorized institutional history; verify reuse is applicable rather than assuming the previous relationship is current. Expand with searches such as `Calgary youth animation nonprofit programs`, `Calgary classroom media community organization accessibility`, and official programme/organization directories. Record date, geography, sources and coverage. Capture an institution's public route only as an option to confirm. [Calgary Public Library partner routes](https://www.calgarylibrary.ca/about-the-library/partnership)
3. Build a longlist from programme or institutional pages. For each, substantiate audience or expertise with one exact source and look for a second source for consequential claims. Verify recency. If they describe reach with no comparable measure, record the exact assertion as unverified. Confirm independent evidence of timing and available staff before recommending commitment.
4. Compare **fit, mutual contribution, equitable access, delivery feasibility, required approvals and risk** with a 0/1/2 scale for weak/partial/strong evidence. Use unknown for unavailable evidence. Keep eligibility, contradictory authority or serious access concerns as explicit decision gates outside any total. Avoid ranking artificial differences. Include one rejected or delayed option with reason.
5. Present up to three candidates, next questions and an allowed public contact route. Keep outreach and offers with the authorized lead. If a candidate proceeds, set approved facts, contribution, credit, rights and who approves each asset. Use MK-08 for embargo, exact versions and receipt.

**Shortlist row** · Candidate | audience/mandate fit with dated sources | mutual value and contribution | evidence strength/unknowns | timing and restrictions | point of contact/approval path | recommendation.

**Worked public-source check, November 16, 2026.** Intended benefit: an elementary classroom can try a hands-on animation method. Quickdraw Animation Society's Portable Animation Workshops page documents classroom workshops and Alberta curriculum-linked packs, so shortlist it for the teaching contribution. Confirm age fit, accessibility, format, availability, fees, recording terms and actual overlap with the current resource before advancing. Calgary Animated Objects Society documents school booking and a puppet-making practice. Keep it as a conditional alternative if the brief shifts to object or shadow storytelling. A past Esker Foundation camp with Quickdraw shows a collaboration model and specifies teens and older participants. It supplies precedent for youth delivery. It leaves this elementary audience match and current capacity unconfirmed. Recommend internal review of the Quickdraw option first, contingent on resolving the existing asset and intended grade scope. No partner was contacted. [Quickdraw workshops](https://quickdrawanimation.ca/learn/portable-animation-workshops) · [Calgary Animated Objects Society school route](https://animatedobjects.ca/contact-us/) · [Puppet practice](https://animatedobjects.ca/puppets/) · [Esker precedent](https://eskerfoundation.com/programs/youth-animation-camp-presented-in-partnership-with-quickdraw-animation-society/)

## MK-06 · Commission and sustain a multi-channel campaign

**Workflow. Input** · scoped service or campaign goal, audience, availability/capacity, calendar, core facts, resources and channel owners. **Return** · versioned campaign brief and placement workback. **Check** · another worker can build a scoped asset, find its verified destination and tell which work can ship first.

1. Identify the primary change and actual service decision. Write one line the team can recognize: “Help [audience] [take action] by [date or trigger], measured by [observable outcome].” Confirm audience, eligibility, availability, access and the earliest partner or newsletter exposure. State what is out of scope.
2. Build a controlling message and fact table, with approved wording or confirmation status for each claim. Give each asset a job: announce/discover, explain, respond, remind or replay. Pick placements based on audience, capacity, confirmed authority and live specifications. The same factual core can support several audience-specific wrappers.
3. Sequence an approved destination before any invite, then pilot/first exposure, amplification, reminder or follow-up, and expiry/maintenance. Work backward from actual receipt and earliest external use. Scope independent assets that can continue under a targeted hold. Assign production, check, authorization, send and receiving roles.
4. Preview each placement in its actual accessible context. Use copy variants that fit audience and action. Link every downstream derivative and partner copy to exact source and version. Use MK-07 for decisions, MK-08 for release and MK-09 for evaluation.

| Placement, when authorized | Distinct audience job | Inspect before ready |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube full form | Answer a durable question or show the whole task | Title/thumbnail and opening promise, captions, playback, description, maintained destination, publisher. |
| YouTube Shorts or Instagram | Earn attention for one meaningful beat or short demonstration | Legibility on the current format, captions, clear next step and platform-specific route. |
| Facebook and partner social | Provide useful local or community context | Actual eligible placement, action and public response/referral owner. |
| LinkedIn | Explain a verified professional, volunteer or community impact angle | Appropriate evidence, credit, quote approval and clear onward route. |
| Email or web | Help a known audience find or complete the action | Approved audience/segment, subject line or heading, current link and accessible preview. |

**Placement row** · Audience/job | message and asset ID/version | platform/owner | handoff date / first audience access / amplification | exact destination | fact/rights/captions | review, send and receipt | expiry or replacement.

**Historical practice signal.** The authorized Practice Inquiry records campaign review, parent/subtask dependencies and a specific approved downloadable item gating a webpage. The brief should carry exact dependency and owner. A previous campaign's cloned tasks are suggestions until each has a current fit, output and capacity.

## MK-07 · Run a kickoff or buy-in decision

**Workflow. Input** · request, applicable MK-01/02 evidence, deadline, audiences and competing constraints. **Return** · pre-read, decision agenda and decision log linked to the workback. **Check** · attendees can explain the decision, rationale, what stays open and the next owner.

1. Decide whether the session is exploratory, decision-bearing, or a readiness check. In an exploratory session, ask open questions and finish with the smallest way to resolve a consequential uncertainty. In a decision session, confirm decision authority and send or prepare an authorized short pre-read.
2. Lead with audience change, exact ask and date, strongest evidence and remaining gap. Compare one recommendation and a credible alternative. Address the production lead's feasibility and resourcing concerns, the marketing lead's audience and destination questions, the communications lead's factual and reputational questions, and the portfolio lead's competition for capacity.
3. At the decision point, read back the exact decision, scope and owner. Record conflicts as they stand, including who will resolve what by when. If the owner is absent, record a recommendation and escalation path. Identify capacities and dependencies before assigning dates. The agent drafts the agenda and log and leaves invitations and commitments to the authorized project lead.
4. Return the log and draft workback together. Each first task has one proposed owner, input, output and evidence for done. Check no decision appears as final solely because attendees agreed a discussion occurred.

**Front page** · Decision and due date | primary audience and change | recommendation and option | dependency/constraint | owner and approvals | next two actions. **Decision log row** · Question | view/evidence | authorized decision or hold | owner/date | affected assets and next step.

## MK-08 · Preflight, release and reconcile variants

**Workflow. Input** · exact campaign package, approvals by scope, placement calendar, destination and receiving requirement. **Return** · approved-scope release handoff and receipt log. **Check** · the receiver can open the exact asset, verify the link and tell what they may do at each milestone.

1. Split three dates: handoff to an internal or partner sender, first intended audience access, and wider amplification. Bind each to version, visibility/access purpose, date/time zone, audience and owner. The practice inquiry found these milestones can be confused in a real event campaign. Route any difference to the person who owns the deadline.
2. Check copy facts and qualifiers, applicability of asset consent or rights, captions and graphics, partner credit/embargo, approved audience and access scope. Preview each link and CTA in representative context. Test newsletter preview with the authorized owner and agreed test route. Mark what required a receiving check you could not observe.
3. Give senders or approvers exact version/destination with restrictions and outstanding decisions. Separate authorized preview/provisional use from final release. Do not treat an unlisted, limited or scheduled route as permission to expose it. Ownership of receiving, publishing and release decisions is project-specific.
4. If a link, fact, cut or approval changes, identify affected caption files, CTA wrappers, scheduled variants, embeds and partner copies. Notify through the actual owner when authorized, retain historical approval scope, and verify receipt of the replacement. Mark a live placement observed from the intended access context only when it actually was. Route incidents to the assigned communications and platform owners.

**Receipt row** · Asset ID/hash or revision | audience/purpose | controlling approval and limits | sender and reviewer | send/first access/amplification | destination QA and time | delivery, access and verified use | correction owner.

## MK-09 · Measure, explain and renew

**Workflow. Input** · prepared tracking plan, comparison, authorized channel and service snapshots, release/version log. **Return** · one-page evaluation and specific renew/revise/pause decision. **Check** · another analyst can reconstruct each figure and understand limits on interpreting it.

1. Build an evidence chain: content delivered → intended people reached → attention or comprehension → action at destination → service participation or public outcome. Set a time window and capture baseline, population, extraction date and metric definition. Keep organic/paid, platform, content type and destination changes visible.
2. Use product reports with their definitions. YouTube's content reports distinguish registered thumbnail impressions, impression CTR and Shorts engaged views. Check applicable scope and window for each format. If combining compatible impression CTR records, use the matching numerator/denominator rather than averaging asset rates. Treat device, distribution and partial-quarter differences as analysis issues, and unavailable data as missing. [YouTube Analytics definitions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?co=YOUTUBE._YT)
3. Diagnose a bottleneck before proposing a change. Low relevant reach with a healthy receiving path points to discoverability testing. Good attention with low action can indicate CTA or destination issues. Good registration with low attendance warrants service-side questions. Longer viewing can indicate either usefulness or confusion. Call out plausible outside contributors such as a partner send, capacity changes or seasonal demand.
4. Return one scoped interpretation and its uncertainty, a fix or next test with owner/date, and a renewal recommendation only when service benefit and workload justify it. For series continuation compare effort, evidence of audience return/use, repeatable topic opportunities and capacity to keep information current.

**Reporting row** · Metric/exact definition | population and period | count/denominator | baseline | source/date | likely limitations | what decision it informs. **Example, illustrative.** 40 clicks from 1,000 compatible eligible impressions and 40 from 4,000 aggregate to 80 from 5,000, or 1.6%. A click rate does not establish programme attendance. Look for separately authorized evidence of completed behaviour before claiming engagement with the service.

## MK-10 · Propose a paid placement pilot

**Skill. Input** · verified service need, audience, owned baseline, proposed capacity and named budget/platform owners. **Return** · uncommitted pilot design with forecast range and preflight. **Check** · the authorized owners can judge whether spend tests a specific assumption and can stop at defined limits.

1. Verify that the hypothesized problem is reach, that the intended destination works and can serve demand, and that a comparable organic or partner intervention is available as a baseline where feasible. Describe the justified audience and placement approach. Use current organizational and platform policy for targeting, privacy, accessibility and locations. Unknown scope remains held for owner confirmation.
2. Draft flight, allowed cap, placements, exclusions, one primary measure with denominator, a low/expected/high range using sourced assumptions, and an operational stop rule for defects, spend or public-service capacity. Test preview, exact destination and tracking in the authorized route. Give the budget and platform owners decision and verification roles. No spending or audience uploading is implied by this packet.
3. After an authorized pilot, compare actuals against baseline, spend/qualified action and delivery constraints. Revisit when opt-in and reporting scope can't support the proposed inference. Keep capacity and accessibility guardrails visible.

**Pilot row** · Hypothesis | baseline and assumptions | placement/period | proposed cap | QA and owner | primary measure | stop rule | decision and verified result.
